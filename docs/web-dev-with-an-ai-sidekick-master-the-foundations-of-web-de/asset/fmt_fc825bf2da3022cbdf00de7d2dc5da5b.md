
For example, GitHub Copilot users completed tasks 55.8% faster in controlled experiments. You can read *The Impact of AI on Developer Productivity: Evidence from GitHub Copilot* paper at the following link: [https://arxiv.org/abs/2302.06590](https://arxiv.org/abs/2302.06590)

Google engineers were about 21% faster in an enterprise trial that you can read about at the following link: [https://arxiv.org/abs/2410.12944](https://arxiv.org/abs/2410.12944)

So the reality of AI-enhanced coding in the current era is messy.

## How AI impacts junior and senior developers differently

This is one of the most revealing things about AI and software development. It looks paradoxical at first, but it actually makes perfect sense once you unpack how juniors and seniors think.

The core idea in one sentence is that AI tends to be most helpful for junior developers because it reduces the friction of writing code and helps close common knowledge gaps. For senior developers, however, the benefits can be less consistent, since AI can sometimes interrupt deeper, expert-levels in sources that you control. Instead of asking general questions and getting answers based on a model's broad training, you upload your own material such as book chapters, documentation, or notes, and the AI works only within that context. This makes NotebookLM particularly effective for studying, since you can treat it as a focused tutor for a specific topic.

A practical routine is to upload a chapter or set of notes, then ask questions like *summarize this section*, *explain this concept in simpler terms*, or *quiz me on the key ideas*.

You can also ask it to compare sections, generate examples, or identify gaps in your understanding. Because every response is tied back to your source material, it reduces the risk of irrelevant or incorrect explanations and helps reinforce what you are actually trying to learn, rather than sending you off into unrelated topics.
