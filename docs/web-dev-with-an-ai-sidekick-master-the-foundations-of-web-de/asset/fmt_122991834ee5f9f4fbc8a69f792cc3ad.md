## Your Learning Style

If you naturally ask:

*   Why this pattern?
*   Why not another?
*   What breaks if I change this?

AI can be patient and available whenever you need to ask another question.

But if your instinct is, *Just give me the solution*, then AI will happily comply, but you’ll finish projects without understanding them. Do not let the tool do your thinking for you.

You need to use AI as an explainer, a reviewer, a sparring partner in decision making, or a debugger, not as the primary author, architect, or decision maker.

Some good phrases to frequently use in your chats with the AI:

*   Please show me minimal examples.
*   What are two alternative approaches?
*   What assumptions am I making?

## Configuring an AI Chatbot for Learning

If you want a free AI chatbot to genuinely help you learn web development (instead of just spitting out code you don’t understand), you need to configure it in three ways:

*   How you start each chat (your learning contract)
*   How you ask questions (prompt patterns)
*   How you verify and practice (so you don’t get tricked by confident nonsense)

Below is a setup that works well with ChatGPT, Gemini, and Claude.

### Create a Reusable Learning Contract Message

At the start of a new chat, paste something like the following learning contract and tweak it to your taste.

Learning contract (paste this at the top of the chat):

```
You are my web dev tutor for HTML, CSS, JavaScript, SQL, Python and Django. Teach me like a beginner who wants to become competent, not like someone who wants a quick copy-paste. Prefer short steps with checkpoints.
When giving code, also explain:
- what the code does
- why this approach is used
- common mistakes
Ask me 1–3 quick questions if needed (but do not stall). Always provide a tiny exercise after explaining a concept.
When I paste an error, help me debug by:
- explaining likely causes
- telling me exactly what to check
- giving the minimal fix first
```
