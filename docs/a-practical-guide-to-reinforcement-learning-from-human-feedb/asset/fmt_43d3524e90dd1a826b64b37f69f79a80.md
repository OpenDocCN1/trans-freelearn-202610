## Evaluation and Challenges in Fine-Tuning

In the previous section on fine-tuning we used accuracy score and f1 score as evaluation metrics for evaluating fine-tuning. By comparing base-line model inference results with fine-tuned model inference, we are able to compare progress gained by fine-tuned model. We will discuss in more detail evaluation metrics and methods in Chapter 11. In this section, we will discuss common challenges in evaluation of fine-tuned models, how `RLHF` helps address some of these challenges, and challenges that still remain to be addressed by `RLHF`.

*   **Metric Selection:** Choosing the right evaluation metrics is complex. Traditional metrics like accuracy and F1 score may not adequately capture the nuances of language generation, such as coherence, relevance, or creativity. For example, the Accuracy and F1 score used for sentiment scoring in previous section, can provide some insights, but they may not be sufficient for a comprehensive evaluation in complex text generation and understanding scenarios. `RLHF` can enhance metric selection by incorporating human feedback directly into the training process, allowing models to optimize for qualities that humans find valuable. This can lead to more nuanced metrics that better reflect real-world performance with human users.

*   **Trade-offs Between Metrics:** Sometimes optimizing one aspect of a model's performance (e.g., fluency) can negatively impact another (e.g., factual accuracy), making it difficult to balance competing objectives. This is where `RLHF` can assist in finding a more favorable balance by allowing human evaluators to prioritize certain traits based on the intended use case, leading to more tailored performance adjustments.

*   **Interpretability:** Understanding why a model performs well or poorly can be challenging, complicating both the evaluation process and efforts to improve the model. `RLHF` can enhance interpretability by making the decision-making process more transparent, as models learn from explicit human feedback about what is considered good or bad performance.

*   **Contextual Sensitivity:** The effectiveness of a model's outputs can vary greatly depending on context, making isolated evaluations less informative. `RLHF` can help models become more context-aware by providing feedback that emphasizes situational relevance and appropriateness, if this type of feedback is collected.

In addition to these challenges, fine-tuned models often exhibit different performance levels across various domains (e.g., medical vs. casual conversation), complicating the evaluation of generalization. This is termed as domain-variability. `RLHF` can help tailor models to specific domains by utilizing human feedback relevant to those contexts, potentially improving performance and generalization within targeted areas. However, it may not fully address performance variability across diverse, unforeseen domains, as the model's training data and feedback are inherently limited to known contexts.
