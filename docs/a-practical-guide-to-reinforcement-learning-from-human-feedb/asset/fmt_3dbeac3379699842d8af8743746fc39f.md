# Importance of Reward in Reinforcement Learning

In previous chapters we introduced RL and the **state-action-reward framework**, where agents interact with their environment to learn optimal decision-making strategies. Now we will focus in more details on the reward itself. The agents decision on selecting actions is based on optimizing a goal, and reward is designed to reach or stay at a goal. When the agent maximizes rewards, it in turn improves its chances of achieving desired goal. Agent training efficiency and ultimately the trained agent’s performance depends on how well the reward is designed.Reward design provides a feedback mechanism for the agent on how well it is doing based on the actions taken, thus driving the learning process. This feedback drives the learning process by incentivizing the agent to explore its environment and refine its behavior to align with desired human objectives. By clearly articulating the desired behaviors through reward assignments, the reward design helps the agent achieve its goals more efficiently and enhances its overall performance. Additionally, clear feedback on actions taken accelerates the agent's learning process. A well-specified reward structure enables the agent to adapt more effectively to its environment. For instance, in a maze navigation task, a robot is more likely to successfully reach a target goal and avoid obstacles if the rewards are designed to guide its actions appropriately.

## Extrinsic and Intrinsic Rewards

**Extrinsic rewards** are assigned when the agent is affected by the environment it is interacting with, such as reaching a goal state or hitting an obstacle. On the contrary, **intrinsic reward** is not related to effects of the environment, but an incentive to behave in a particular way. Considering the example of grid world in *Chapter 1*, reaching a goal state was assigned a reward of *+10* and hitting an obstacle was assigned a reward of *\-5*, both of which are extrinsic rewards based on environmental interaction. Similarly, a penalty of taking a step by assigning a reward of *\-1* signals the agent to take least number of steps to reach a goal, and since this reward is directly related to completing the task efficiently, an environmental entity, it is a shaped extrinsic reward. Conversely, a behavioral incentive can be provided by encouraging exploration and curiosity e.g., *+0.5* for visiting a new cell, and it is an intrinsic reward as it only incentivizes agent behavior and is independent of environment.

## Reward Shaping and How it Accelerates Training

**Reward Shaping** is the process of modifying the reward function to provide additional, intermediate rewards that guide the agent towards more efficient learning. Providing **intermediate rewards** is a technique of **reward shaping** where we introduce rewards for achieving sub-goals or milestones within a task, signaling progress towards reaching optimal **policy**. A complementary technique of reward shaping is assigning directional rewards. Directional rewards are rewards given based on the agent's progress or movement toward the main goal. Both **intermediate rewards** and **directional rewards** accelerate agent training. While **intermediate rewards** focus on providing structured feedback for achieving sub-goals, **directional rewards** provide continuous feedback based on the agent's progress toward the main goal. Using both techniques can help agents learn more effectively by providing clear and frequent guidance throughout the learning process. In a grid world, if the main goal is to reach the bottom-right corner, **intermediate rewards** might be given for reaching specific intermediate cells or checkpoints. These intermediate cells may not be closest to the main goal, but might be useful for avoiding an obstacle. In contrast, a **directional reward** might be given for moving closer to the goal e.g., *+0.5* for each step closer to the goal) and a penalty for moving away e.g., *\-0.15* for each step away from the goal).While intermediate rewards and directional rewards focus on performance and achieving a goal, exploitation of the knowledge, for complex environments with unknown regions, it makes sense to explore the environment also. To encourage exploration, rewarding the agent for exploring new states or actions, promoting comprehensive learning is a sensible strategy. This can be done by assigning **exploration bonuses** to the agent.In context of reward signals for training an agent, reward specification or design has direct impact on training performance of the agent. While reward shaping can accelerate training, some level of deliberation and care is required for designing the reward functions. Reward signals need to be informative. **Informativeness** in this context means how much useful information the reward signal provides to the agent about how to improve its behavior (defined in the *Explicable Reward Design for Reinforcement Learning Agents*: https://proceedings.neurips.cc/paper/2021/file/a7f0d2b95c60161b3f3c82f764b1d1c9-Paper.pdf )\[1\]. More informative rewards give the agent clearer guidance on which actions are beneficial and which are not. A related concept is **sparseness**, which refers to how frequently rewards are given. Sparse rewards are given infrequently, often only for major accomplishments, while dense rewards are given more frequently for smaller achievements. If rewards are too sparse, the agent might struggle to learn the policy because it receives insufficient feedback to guide its behavior. If rewards are too dense but not informative, the agent might learn suboptimal behaviors or take longer to understand the most effective strategies. Therefore, a balanced approach is necessary. By balancing **informativeness** and **sparseness**, the reward structure can provide frequent, meaningful feedback that helps the agent learn efficiently and effectively. While there are practices for achieving this balance, they are very case specific, and considerable skill and domain knowledge starts becoming necessary. We will discuss the challenges in specifying rewards in more details next.

# Challenges in Reward Specification

Reward Specification and Design is critical for agent training, but it has several challenges. We will discuss some of them in this section, and understand what alternatives could help overcome them.

## Misaligned Objectives

Misalignment between the objectives that agent learns and human intent can emanate from ambiguous rewards. Ambiguous rewards can lead to unintended behaviors. It can be difficult to precisely specify the desired behaviors and outcomes through rewards. Another factor can be due to the task complexity itself. In complex tasks involving multiple goals, it can be challenging to capture the objectives accurately in a single reward function.

## Sparse and Delayed Rewards

In many environments, rewards are sparse and delayed or both. Sparse reward cause exploration challenges due to infrequent feedback of the success of actions. While reward shaping techniques can help with some of the sparseness issues, and appear myopically optimal learning \[1\] as detailed in *Explicable Reward Design for Reinforcement Learning Agents*: https://proceedings.neurips.cc/paper/2021/file/a7f0d2b95c60161b3f3c82f764b1d1c9-Paper.pdf, delayed reward can create confusion success of current action and reward.
