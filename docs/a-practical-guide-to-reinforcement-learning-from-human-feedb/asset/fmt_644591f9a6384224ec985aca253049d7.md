
## Incorporating Human Preferences into RL Training

Incorporating human preferences and signals into RL training process or architecture involves several steps, as outlined in *Figure 2.2*. The first step is how the human preferences are collected—are they collected as binary inputs per step or per episode of the agent training, or a segment of trajectory of sequences of agent? Are humans using choices of multiple actions or resulting states or trajectories of states? Next step is to decide on how the human preferences are utilized to guide the development of agent’s policy. Is policy updated directly or value function updated? Is reward function modified based on human preferences? Or, is a reward model built and trained, and then used for training the agent? The third and final step of incorporating human preferences is related to ethical considerations and safety. Processes mitigating bias, improving fairness, maintaining transparency and ensuring long-term alignment of AI with human need to be build and integrated into the training flow. We will analyze each step in the following subsections.

### Collecting Human Preferences

Collection of human signals or preferences involves choice from implicit set of options that the human can make, and a function or model that maps these choices made by human to agent behavior. The type of human feedback or choice is termed **reward-rational implicit choice**, if given a grounding function it can be modeled as a choice that maximizes reward as elaborated her Reward-rational (implicit) choice: A unifying formalism for reward learning : `https://proceedings.neurips.cc/paper/2020/hash/2f10c1578a0706e06b6d7db6f0b4a6af-Abstract.xhtml`. The form of human feedback can be many: comparison, demonstration, correction, improvement, correction, improvement, turning off, proxy rewards, reward and punishment (similar to what we used in previous chapter), initial state and credit assignments. The choice depends on the feedback type, for example during comparison, identity of feedback input chosen is enough, while during proxy reward, value of reward output is used, and a positive or negative assignment in case of reward and punishment type feedback.

### Utilizing Human Preferences

The collected human preference can be used for guiding the policy using some utility, map or a grounding function. ### Evaluating and Auditing Human Preferences

Evaluating and auditing human preferences in RL systems is crucial for ensuring that the system accurately reflects user values and operates fairly and effectively. Assessing how well the RL system incorporates human preferences and identifying potential areas for improvement, involves ensuring accuracy RL system by logging human preferences and how they are used within the training process. Tracking performance metrics is crucial for evaluating the effectiveness of an RL system. Key metrics include:

*   **Alignment Metrics**: These measure how well the RL system’s decisions align with user preferences. This can be assessed through:
    *   Preference Satisfaction Scores
    *   User Satisfaction Ratings
    *   Adherence to Stated Preferences
*   **Diversity Metrics**: These evaluate the range of preferences accommodated by the system. Ensuring diversity is important for handling a wide array of user values and can be measured by examining:
    *   The variety of user preferences satisfied
    *   The system’s ability to cater to different user values

In addition to tracking metrics, regular audits can be conducted for in-production systems. This can include conducting periodic reviews with automated checks and human oversight. Engaging independent third parties to conduct unbiased audits of the system can provide an external perspective and enhance credibility. Feedback mechanisms such as ongoing user feedback can help identify any discrepancies between their preferences, the system’s actions, and inputs used for training (e.g. reward model). This may involve developing interactive platforms, where users can interactively report issues, suggest improvements, and provide direct feedback on the system’s decisions.

## Bias and Other Challenges

Human feedback, while valuable in RLHF (reinforcement learning with human feedback), can introduce various challenges, inaccuracies and biases. These can be broadly categorized into technological and ethical challenges and can be handled in technical and legal ways. Human preferences are inherently subjective, varying greatly between individuals. What one person values highly, another might consider unimportant. Even for the same person, preferences can change over time, influenced by new experiences, changing contexts, and evolving needs. Hence, it is necessary to do a thorough evaluation of human preferences, and integrate upon considering these imperfections in human signals. Some ways of handling diversity of human preferences are:

*   Aggregating multiple preferences using consensus methods such as majority voting, average ranking, or weighted scoring to aggregate preferences from multiple users, aiming to find a balance that reflects a broader consensus.
*   Grouping users with similar preferences into clusters and create tailored RL policies for each group.
*   Designing RL algorithms that can perform well across a range of possible preference scenarios, by creating policies that are not overly sensitive to specific preference variations.
*   Apply regularization techniques to prevent overfitting to specific preferences. This helps in creating more generalized policies that can adapt to varying preferences. For example, in `[2301.11774]` Reinforcement Learning from Diverse Human Preferences: `https://arxiv.org/abs/2301.11774`, reward learning is stabilized by regularizing and also correcting predictions in latent space where inputs of reward models are mapped, applying a strong distribution constraint to inhibit inconsistencies in preferences.
*   Implement systems that continually learn and adapt to changing preferences through ongoing feedback loops. This ensures that the RL system remains aligned with user preferences even as they evolve. One example would be to create user interfaces and telemetry data collection processed that allow anonymized user activity and feedback to be collected, in order to update the data used for reward modeling. This would help retrain or fine-tune reward models to out-of-distribution data, if user preferences change.
*   Using models that can dynamically update based on new inputs, allowing the RL system to remain flexible and responsive to changes in user preferences.
