## Transfer Learning to 8x8 Grid

For transfer learning (TL), the Q-values from the **6x6** environment are transferred to a more complex but similar environment with **8 x 8** cells and **5** obstacles (instead of 3). Here we compare training the agent from scratch and training with transferred Q-values by comparing their policy progress. Note that we don't compare the policy progress for TL with Q-learning from scratch for the **6x6** environment, but instead train the **8 x 8** environment with **5** obstacles again using the `q_learning_scratch` method. Execution code is as follows:

```
obstacle_positions_5 = [(2, 3), (3, 1), (4, 3), (5, 2), (2, 5)]
new_grid_world = GridWorld(grid_size=(8,8), obstacle_positions=obstacle_positions_5)
```

# Reinforcement Learning from Human Feedback

In the preceding section, we saw that training an agent by transferring Q-values pretrained on an environment (**6 x 6** grid with **3** obstacles) similar to the target environment (**8 x 8** grid with **5** obstacles) can be effective in speeding up the learning progress. There is an important caveat: the environment should have reasonable similarity for benefiting from the policies learned. If the environment is significantly different, or worse—conflicting in goals (or reward incentives to reach goals)—transfer learning may prove to be ineffective. I suggest you experiment with obstacles and goals that are very different from those used in getting the Q-values of the policy to be transferred. How do we accelerate training in such scenarios? One approach is to incorporate human feedback into the training of the policy. Human feedback can take various forms, such as explicit rewards, evaluations, or demonstrations of desired behavior.

- **Explicit Rewards:** The human feedback provides explicit rewards or penalties based on the agent's actions. For example, in the `gridworld` scenario, the human might assign positive rewards for moving towards a goal and negative rewards for hitting obstacles.

- **Evaluations:** The human evaluates the agent's actions or policies and provides feedback based on their performance. For instance, they might provide feedback like "That was a good move" or "You should have gone left instead of right," objective thumbs up or down, "yes" or "no".

- **Demonstrations:** The human demonstrates the desired behavior by showing the agent how to solve tasks in the environment. This can be in the form of trajectories or sequences of actions leading to successful outcomes.
