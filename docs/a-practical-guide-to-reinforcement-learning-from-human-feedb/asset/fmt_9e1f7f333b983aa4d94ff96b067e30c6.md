
# Direct Preference Optimization (DPO)

**Direct Preference Optimization** (**DPO**) represents a significant advancement in language model alignment, offering a more elegant and efficient alternative to traditional **Reinforcement Learning from Human Feedback** (**RLHF**). At its core, DPO learns directly from pairwise human preference data—bypassing the need for an explicit reward model or complex reinforcement learning machinery. The foundational insight behind DPO is intuitive: when presented with two responses to a prompt—one preferred by a human and one rejected—the model should be updated to increase the probability of generating the preferred response, while decreasing the likelihood of the rejected one. This is achieved through a contrastive learning framework that treats preference comparisons as a binary classification problem.

The DPO training pipeline is streamlined and efficient:

- **Data Collection**: Human annotators provide feedback in the form of triplets: (prompt, preferred response, rejected response).
- **Model Initialization**: The policy model is initialized from a pre-trained checkpoint, typically one that has undergone **supervised fine-tuning** (**SFT**).
- **Reference Model Anchoring**: A frozen copy of the initial model is used as a reference to regularize updates, preventing the policy from diverging too far from its base behavior.
- **Contrastive Preference Optimization**: The model is fine-tuned using a specialized loss function that encourages it to assign higher log-likelihoods to preferred responses relative to rejected ones.

One of DPO’s key innovations is that it reframes the reward maximization objective in RLHF as a more tractable binary classification task over human preferences. This reformulation leads to a training objective that is simpler to implement, more stable to optimize, and often more efficient in practice.

## How Human Preferences Are Used Directly to Shape the Policy

In traditional RLHF, human preferences are first converted into scalar reward values via a learned reward model. The policy is then trained to maximize expected returns using reinforcement learning. **Direct Preference Optimization (DPO)** removes this intermediate step entirely. Instead, it leverages preference data directly, treating pairwise comparisons as **contrastive supervision signals**.

For each prompt, human annotators provide a preference between two candidate responses: the **preferred response** and the **rejected response**. The objective is to update the policy `π` so that it assigns higher likelihood to the preferred response.
