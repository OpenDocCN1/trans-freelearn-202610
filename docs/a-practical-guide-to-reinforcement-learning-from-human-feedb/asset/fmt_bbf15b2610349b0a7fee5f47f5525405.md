
In line with developments in batch reinforcement learning, `Reinforced Self-Training (ReST)` begins with selecting an initial large language model (`LLM`) as the base policy. This is followed by the **Grow** step, where the base policy is used to generate a large and diverse set of synthetic data. These samples are then evaluated and scored using a reward model—often employing tree search methods—to filter for high-quality data. The **Improve** step uses this filtered dataset in an offline reinforcement learning setting to optimize the policy. These Grow and Improve steps are repeated iteratively to achieve further improvements. `ReST` offers greater efficiency than typical online `RLHF` methods by enabling data reuse and minimizing the need for expensive online interactions. While this approach is broadly applicable to generative learning tasks, it has shown particular promise in machine translation, significantly improving output quality in a compute- and sample-efficient manner. However, `ReST` comes with limitations. It depends on the availability of large, high-quality offline datasets, and training is driven by offline RL losses—which inherit known issues such as limited exploration. Additionally, the reward model may struggle to generalize effectively beyond the training distribution. For a deeper dive, refer to the Reinforced Self-Training (ReST) for Language Modeling: [https://arxiv.org/pdf/2308.08998](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2308.08998). Overall, `ReST` represents a fundamentally different paradigm from standard `RLHF` architectures and is still an emerging technique. The next class of techniques moves beyond traditional reinforcement learning altogether, focusing instead on principle-driven objectives and learning from minimal supervision.

### Principle Driven and Minimal Supervision Approaches

While traditional alignment approaches often require extensive human feedback or ranking, recent advances demonstrate that **language models can be aligned using self-generated data and principle-driven reasoning**, with minimal human supervision. These methods rely on **explicitly written rules or principles** as alignment anchors and use the generative and evaluative capabilities of the `LLM` itself to simulate feedback, train responses, and refine outputs. By leveraging models' internal reasoning and self-supervision loops, these approaches reduce reliance on large-scale preference datasets and complex reinforcement learning pipelines. Notable approaches in this category include **`SELF-ALIGN`** and **`UltraFeedback`**. While both reduce human annotation needs, their underlying training methods differ: `SELF-ALIGN` avoids reinforcement learning altogether, whereas `UltraFeedback` enhances `RLHF` using scaled AI feedback instead of human rankings. These methodologies represent a new direction in scalable alignment, where **interpretability, data reuse, and synthetic supervision** drive the fine-tuning process. These approaches provide rule based governance that is a set of principles that can be used for feedback generation and iterative refinement.

#### SELF-ALIGN Methodology

`SELF-ALIGN` combines principle-driven reasoning with the generative capabilities of `LLMs` to create self-aligned AI agents with minimal human supervision. This is a four-stage approach and includes:

**Synthetic Prompt Generation**  
A base model generates a wide set of prompts using *topic-guided sampling*. This ensures diversity across conversational domains like ethics, emotion, or instruction-following. Techniques such as self-instruct are used for prompt augmentation, providing broad coverage without manual curation.

**Principle-Based Feedback via In-Context Learning**  
Instead of manually scoring outputs, the model is prompted with a small number of *human-written principles* (e.g., "Be helpful," "Avoid biased language") and shown examples of applying them to responses. It then generates its own responses or revises previous ones, acting as both respondent and critic. This leverages the model's internal reasoning abilities for alignment.

**Fine-Tuning with Self-Aligned Outputs**  
High-quality responses generated in the previous step—those that best reflect the stated principles—are selected and used to fine-tune the base model. This helps move the model's default behavior toward outputs that conform to human-aligned values, even without explicit human rankings.

**Iterative Refinement**  
After fine-tuning, additional passes are done to fix common response issues (e.g., vagueness, over-cautiousness). This might involve new principle-driven critiques or structured feedback asking the model to revise for clarity, depth, or tone, further polishing the outputs. Applied to `LLaMA-65b`, this approach created `Dromedary`, an AI assistant requiring fewer than 300 lines of human annotations yet outperforming several systems including `Text-Davinci-003` and `Alpaca`, but could not surpass `ChatGPT`. The approach shows that *interpretable, low-cost alignment* is possible through principled, self-supervised refinement—though additional safeguards are required to ensure robustness against adversarial prompts. This approach is detailed in the paper, Principle-Driven Self-Alignment of Language Models from Scratch with Minimal Human Supervision: [https://arxiv.org/abs/2305.03047](https://arxiv.org/abs/2305.03047), and while promising, requires further research.

#### UltraFeedback

**`UltraFeedback`** complements `RLHF` by replacing human-written preference scores with high-quality, AI-generated feedback—often from models like `GPT-4`. This approach scales the reward modeling process by **automating critique and preference comparisons**, enabling larger training datasets without requiring extensive human review. It demonstrates that carefully constructed feedback mechanisms can drive significant performance improvements without extensive human annotation. This represents an efficient pathway to creating more aligned systems at scale. `UltraFeedback` focuses on key premise that scale and diversity of samples can improve eventual preference and alignment. It still uses the `PPO` framework from `RLHF`, but replaces the expensive human annotation phase with curated, diverse, and principled model feedback. This aligns the model more efficiently, especially when paired with reward modeling techniques like Direct Preference Optimization (`DPO`, discussed in following chapter 10) or advanced filtering strategies. Using the top models on leaderboard such as `GPT-4` to critique user-assistant interactions across four key aspects - instruction-following, truthfulness, honesty, and helpfulness, enables the collection of diverse datasets that can be used for training scalable reward models. Two of such models are **`UltraRM-13B`** ([https://huggingface.co/openbmb/UltraRM-13b](https://huggingface.co/openbmb/UltraRM-13b)) and **`UltraCM-13b`** ([https://huggingface.co/openbmb/UltraCM-13b](https://huggingface.co/openbmb/UltraCM-13b)), these are critic models trained on AI based critique. The critic models of `Ultra Feedback`—diverse instruction sampling, multi-model response generation, and fine-grained `GPT-4` grading—offers a reference pipeline for scalable `RLAIF`. It trains the AI models using 1M+ AI critiques—orders of magnitude larger than typical `RLHF`/`RLAIF` datasets, typically 100k samples. This proves `RLAIF`'s viability for cost-effective, large-scale alignment. You can read more about in their paper, ULTRAFEEDBACK: Boosting Language Models with Scaled AI Feedback: [https://arxiv.org/pdf/2310.01377](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2310.01377). Both `SELF-ALIGN` and `UltraFeedback` reflect the growing trend toward **data-efficient and interpretable alignment methods**. However, they differ in execution:

*   **`SELF-ALIGN`** eliminates reinforcement learning and relies entirely on instruction fine-tuning with synthetic, principle-compliant data.
*   **`UltraFeedback`** preserves `PPO`-based `RLHF`, but drastically reduces human involvement through automated feedback generation.
