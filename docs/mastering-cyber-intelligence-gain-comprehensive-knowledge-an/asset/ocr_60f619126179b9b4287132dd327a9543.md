+   - Enhances the system's capability to detect threats thanks to its power to correlate data from internal and external sources, generate real-time contextualized alerts when a malicious IOC is discovered, and map IOCs to adversaries groups, campaigns, and TTPs.
+   - Supports IR by speeding up the IR process phases such as preparation, triage, analyses, and remediation. It also provides incident scope by correlating internal data to external data feeds.
+   - Facilitates threat hunting to discover unknown threats.

There are several SIEM tools on the market. The choice depends entirely on the organization's budget and requirements. Some of the popular SIEM tools include **Datadog Security Monitoring**, **Splunk Enterprise**, **IBM QRadar**, **AlienVault USM**, **SolarWinds SEM**, and **McAfee ESM**. Note that the list is not exclusive. There are many other commercial SIEM tools.

Integrating threat intelligence into SIEM must be a priority for any organization that wants to enhance security defense. Its use cases do not stop at SOC and IR. It extends to vulnerability and risk management, and fraud management. It also supports security leadership activities by guiding the right security strategy and investment. So, when investing in SIEM, ensure that threat intelligence capability is integrated into the tool.
