In this example, we'll use our understanding of straight lines and intuition to update the parameters for each iteration manually. Our line is too shallow, and the intercept is too low; therefore, we must increase both values. We can control the updates we make each iteration by choosing a _step size_. We must update the _m_ and _c_ values with each iteration by adding the step size. The results, for a step size of 0.1, is shown in Table 1.2.

| Guess# | m | c | MAE |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | 0.1 | 4 | 4.61 |
| 2 | 0.2 | 4.1 | 3.89 |
| 3 | 0.3 | 4.2 | 3.17 |
| 4 | 0.3 | 4.3 | 2.5 |
| 5 | 0.4 | 4.4 | 1.83 |

Table 1.2 – Step wise guessing of the slope (**_m_**) and y-intercept (**_c_**) for a straight line to fit our data. The quality of fit is measured using the MAE

In our example, the _step size_ is a _hyperparameter_ of our training process.

We end up with an error of 1.83, which means, on average, our predictions are wrong by less than 2,000.
