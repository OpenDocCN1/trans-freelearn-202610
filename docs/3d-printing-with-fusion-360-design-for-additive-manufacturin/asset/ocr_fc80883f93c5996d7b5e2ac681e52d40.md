If we had chosen the first option, we could have left the shape optimization outcome as a mesh body and simply tessellated the base and the mounting brackets on the left- and right-hand sides. Then, we could have combined all four bodies as mesh objects and achieved a similar outcome. However, if we had utilized the mesh-based workflow and proceeded to create our final design as a mesh object, even though we would be able to 3D print our final design, we would not be able to simulate it using linear static stress within Fusion 360. This is because Fusion 360 requires objects to be solid bodies for them to be simulated. *Figure 3.30* shows our final design from four different angles. In this finished version, I have also moved all the unnecessary components into a new sub-assembly called **Extras** and turned their visibility off.
