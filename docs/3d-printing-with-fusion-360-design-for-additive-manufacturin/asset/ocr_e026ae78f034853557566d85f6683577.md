The next result type we will highlight is the recoater clearance. During the printing process, as the laser moves over the powder, the metal powder melts and then solidifies. As the metal heats and cools during this cycle, it experiences thermally induced stresses. During the printing process, the layer that was just printed will experience deformations in the X, Y, and Z directions. If a given layer deforms in the positive Z axis an amount above the layer thickness we set within our print settings, our part will experience a recoater crash. Fusion 360 can predict such phenomena using process simulation. The recoater clearance percentage result type allows us to visualize how close our part is to the recoater blade during the printing process. A 100% clearance means our part is not experiencing any Z deformation at a given point. A 0% clearance means that our part is experiencing a Z deformation equivalent to the layer thickness. Fusion 360 displays the summary of this result for all layers in a single result type called **Global Recoater Clearance**, as shown in Figure 13.15. A clearance value above 100% refers to a given point experiencing a negative Z-direction deformation.
