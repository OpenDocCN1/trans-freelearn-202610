WordPress is one of the most popular CMSes out there. It powers roughly 40% of all websites available on the internet. You can find more information about WordPress at https://wordpress.org/. For a list of the most widely used CMSes, refer to Wikipedia at https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_content_management_systems.

Going headless means that the frontend (the public-facing part) and the backend (the administrative area) are *decoupled*. In other words, they can be two completely different systems using different technologies, being hosted on two distinct IT infrastructures and using two completely different domain names.

In other words, going *headless* means that you can design and develop a custom website hosted on any domain but whose *content* (partially) comes from Captivate Prime and is entirely managed through the various administrative areas of Prime.

At the time of this writing, Captivate Prime is one of the very few LMSes on the market to offer such capability. To make it possible, Captivate Prime offers the following two features:

- You can embed fluid player into your website using a technique that is similar to how a YouTube video is embedded on a web page.
- Captivate Prime offers a comprehensive **Application Programming Interface (API)**. This means that the developer of your website can remotely query Captivate Prime for content, and then display that content within your (existing) website. Your website can also use the Prime API to post data (such as users' progress in courses, quiz and survey results, submitted assignments, and so on) to the Captivate Prime backend.

If this sounds rather technical to you, don't worry! Implementing a headless LMS is the job of a developer. It has been successfully done by many Captivate Prime customers. As a Prime user or administrator, you should not worry about the nitty-gritty details of implementing a headless LMS.

The main takeaway of this section is that the course content and all learning interactions managed by Captivate Prime can be delivered to the learner through your own custom website.

## Getting content into Captivate Prime
Adobe Captivate Prime is an LMS. A basic way to define an LMS is to say that it is where learning content, learners, and instructors meet. In other words, getting learning content into Captivate Prime is paramount.

Unlike many other LMSes (especially those LMSes targeted at the education market), Captivate Prime does not have any built-in, browser-based, content authoring capabilities. This means that all the content hosted on your Captivate Prime account has to be brought in from outside the system.

Basically, the content that's available to your learners can come from two types of sources:

- You can integrate third-party, off-the-shelf content libraries into Prime.
- You can upload your own custom learning content into the platform.

Let's take a closer, high-level look at these two options.

## Integrating third-party, off-the-shelf content
There are a lot of learning content providers out there, and it is not uncommon for organizations to subscribe to external learning content libraries to provide their workforce access to a wealth of learning resources.

Some of the most famous providers are LinkedIn Learning (previously known as Lynda.com), Udemy, OpenClassrooms, and Pluralsight. These suppliers provide tens of thousands of courses in many languages covering a wide range of topics. From business (leadership, management, personal development, communication, and so on) to technology (network administration, data science, programming languages, web development, and so on), from office automation (Microsoft Office, SharePoint, Open Office, Adobe Acrobat, and so on) to creativity (3D modeling, animation, design, video editing, and so on), a growing number of employees enjoy access to this vast array of learning material through their organizations' subscriptions. Increasingly, access to this type of content is seen as part of the standard package provided by employers.

But organizations do not offer access to such content just for the sake of it! They want return on investment, which means they need data out of this training effort. For example, they want to know which employees are actively engaged in learning new skills so that these employees can be rewarded with a pay raise, or by being promoted to a higher position. Organizations also want to make sure each employee has spent some time upskilling him/herself so that everyone stays up to date in their respective roles, or that the required certifications and compliance training have been effectively and successfully taken by each employee, and so on.
