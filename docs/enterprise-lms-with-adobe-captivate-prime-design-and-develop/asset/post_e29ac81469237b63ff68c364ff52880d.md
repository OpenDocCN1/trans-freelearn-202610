## Adding alternative delivery types to modules

There are many instances where it is helpful to be able to deliver the same modules using different delivery types, as shown by the following examples:

- - A specific instructor is out on medical leave for a few months, so his/her instructor-led classes have been temporarily replaced with self-paced modules (recordings of previous classes, for example).
- - You may not have instructors who can conduct live or virtual classes in every language, so for some languages, you must provide a self-paced module (for example, a recording of the live class with subtitles in different languages) instead of the instructor-led class itself.
- - In large organizations, learners may be spread all over the world, so you need to take cultural differences into account when designing your course. For example, in my country (Belgium), learners tend to favor instructor-led classes over self-paced modules (this is partly because Belgium is a tiny country – Belgium is a little bit *smaller* than the state of Maryland – so attending live training, even if it is located in the other side of the country, is usually not a problem). It is much different in bigger countries such as the United States, for example.
- - Various local regulations may require that certain modules of your course be expanded in some parts of the world and not in others. What fits a 3-minute self-paced module in a country may require a 3-hour webinar in another country.
- - You may simply want to provide the best possible learning experience for all learners, regardless of their learning styles.
- - Etc.

For all these reasons, plus the ones not listed here, Captivate Prime allows you to define alternative delivery types for your modules. In *Chapter 6, Enrolling Learners to Courses*, you will learn about **course instances**, which is where you will be able to decide which delivery type is used for which instance of the course.

### Using Prework modules

Prework modules are modules designed to help learners prepare for the course. This type of learning content is often used in the following situations:

- - Adding self-paced modules explaining how Captivate Prime works from the learner's perspective.
- - Adding a video to introduce the course author(s) and/or instructor(s).
- - Providing an optional live or virtual class to introduce the course.
- - Providing some material to help learners determine if they meet the course prerequisites or if they have the correct skills or work experience before they take the training.

When adding Prework modules to a course, keep in mind that learners are not required to view these modules to complete the course and earn credits. This is the main difference between regular Content modules and Prework modules: Content modules are required to complete the course, while Prework modules are not required to complete the course.

The process of adding Prework modules is the same as the process of adding regular Content modules: click the Add Modules link and select the modules you want to add. Note that you can add the same module types as for regular Content modules: Self Paced Modules, Virtual Classroom Modules, Classroom Modules, and Activity Modules.

Feel free to add one or more Prework module(s) to your course before moving on to the next section.

### Using Testout modules

Staff training costs time and money, so organizations want to ensure that proper training is being provided to those individuals that need it. In large organizations, it is not uncommon to enroll large groups of people in training. While this practice makes it easier to administer the LMS, it also increases the risk of enrolling someone who already knows the subject matter in a course, thus wasting that person's time and the organization's money.

Another use case for Testout modules is within the government space, where many courses require yearly attendance, even if the material has not changed. One possible way to help the learner, and the LMS owners, is to offer Testouts. This helps save the learner's time, as well as provide data that proves topic mastery.

Learners may also want to validate the corporate LMS skills they have acquired elsewhere (in a previous job, at school, while traveling, in their hobbies, and so on).
