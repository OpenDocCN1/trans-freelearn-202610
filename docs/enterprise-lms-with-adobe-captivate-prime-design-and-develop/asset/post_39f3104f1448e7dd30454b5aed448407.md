
This is where a platform such as Captivate Prime comes into play. By integrating this external content into the LMS, Prime can track each interaction with the learning material and generate meaningful reports out of this data, allowing managers to make informed decisions. Prime is also able to use its AI-powered recommendation engine to mine these massive repositories of training content, and then provide a customized list of highly relevant recommendations that match the current industry standards and requirements of each learner.

To make this integration possible, Captivate Prime provides a handful of connectors out of the box. Lots of these providers also offer APIs that your development team can use to make the content available as a course catalog in your Prime account.

## Uploading your own custom content

In addition to the external content discussed in the previous section, most organizations also need to train their workforce on specific topics relevant to their specific business, regulations, workflows, and tools. Another use case is when an organization wants to train external users such as providers, contractors, or even customers on their products, workflows, tools, and so on. That's why Captivate Prime also offers you the ability to upload your own custom course material.

Remember that Captivate Prime does not include any built-in authoring tools, so this custom content must be produced outside of Prime.

## Conclusion

Learners using your Prime account have access to both the external content provided by third-party providers and the custom content produced by your own internal instructional designers. Both types of content are tracked by the system, and both types of content are used by the AI-powered recommendation engine to generate custom recommendations for each learner. This allows Captivate Prime to fit into an increasingly widening range of organizations and use cases.

Integrating external content is made possible through a handful of connectors available out of the box, as well as through APIs that your IT and dev teams can leverage to integrate that content into your Prime account. Custom content must be made outside of Prime because Captivate Prime does not have any built-in authoring tools.

## Modules, courses, and learning programs

When you upload a file to the Content Library, you create what Captivate Prime calls a **Module**. This module is the smallest content unit available in Prime. In other words, each video tutorial, Word document, PDF file, PowerPoint presentation, and SCORM package that you upload to Prime is considered a **Module**.

Note that the modules you upload to Prime are all *asynchronous* learning activities. As discussed in *Chapter 1, Introduction to Adobe Captivate Prime*, an asynchronous learning activity is some learning content that each learner is free to review at his/her own pace and convenience. *Synchronous* activities, on the other end, are learning activities where multiple learners are connected at the same time. Typical *synchronous* learning activities are virtual classes, as well as traditional classroom instructions.

These modules can be arranged into **Courses**. From that perspective, a Captivate Prime course is just a series of modules bundled together. While this definition of a course is enough to get you started, in the next chapter, *Creating Skills and Courses*, you will discover that a course is a little bit more than that as it can also contain *synchronous* activities not included in the Content Library.

Going up one more step, courses can further be arranged into **Learning Programs**. A learning program is a bundle of courses that complement each other. The following diagram summarizes the basic elements of the content hierarchy that prevails in Captivate Prime:

## A first look at the Content Library

The Content Library is where all the asynchronous course modules are available in your Prime account live. By default, every course module ends up in the Content Library, regardless of which author uploaded a particular file.
