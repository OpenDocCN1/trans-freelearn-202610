## 14 Working with Catalogs and Peer Accounts

As the enterprise LMS of choice, Adobe Captivate Prime understands that no two organizations are alike! Each has its own projects, its own structure, its own corporate culture, and its own strategy. That's why Captivate Prime was designed with flexibility in mind so that it can fit into the widest possible variety of organizations and industries, each with their own requirements.

For example, currently, all of the users defined on your Prime account have access to all of the courses available in your course catalog. This is the default situation, and it might very well meet your needs. However, more often than not, organizations need precise control over who can access which courses or need to segment content and reporting data for certain user groups. For example, courses available to internal users might be different from courses available to external users; courses that contain sensitive or confidential information might only be made available to specific audiences within the organization. These are just two of the many situations where not all learners should have access to the same set of courses. In Captivate Prime, such access control is achieved by creating multiple course catalogs.

Besides allowing you to define who can access which courses, creating catalogs also allows you to share your courses with other Prime accounts, which is very useful in many situations, such as when you sell courses to other organizations or when the headquarters of a multinational corporation wants to share courses with local offices from around the world.

In addition to sharing course content, Captivate Prime also allows you to share user seats with other accounts using the Peer Accounts feature. As discussed in the *Getting Captivate Prime* section of *Chapter 1, Introduction to Adobe Captivate Prime*, Captivate Prime is billed on a per-seat basis. With peer accounts, an organization can purchase a large number of seats and distribute them among the Prime accounts of its regional subsidiaries. Additionally, organizations can use peer accounts to fund training for their partners or franchisees. In this case, Prime is purchased by the organization and the user seats are distributed among the peer accounts used by these partners and franchisees. This allows large organizations to centralize the cost of using Captivate Prime (not to mention that purchasing a large number of seats usually results in better rates) while keeping an eye on the overall training and development strategy of the entire organization.

These are the features you will explore in this chapter, which includes the following main sections:

- Working with multiple catalogs
- Sharing catalogs
- Enabling and disabling catalogs
- Catalogs in the learner experience
- Exploring the Content Marketplace
- Working with peer accounts

With these features, you will be able to tailor your Captivate Prime deployment to exactly match the needs of your organization, regardless of its size, industry, strategy, or internal operation.

### Working With Multiple Catalogs

In Captivate Prime, a catalog is a collection of learning objects. So far, in this book, all the content available on your account is part of the default catalog that was automatically created along with your Prime account. This default catalog has the following characteristics:

- It cannot be deleted (but it can be disabled instead; more on that in the Enabling and disabling catalogs section of this chapter).
- All the courses, learning programs, certifications, and job aids that you create on your Prime account are automatically included in the default catalog.
- By default, all the learners defined on your account (including internal and external learners) have access to the default catalog and, therefore, to all the content available on your Prime account.

This default situation is very simple to understand and administer, and it may well correspond to your needs. There is nothing wrong with using it in production. However, there are also many situations where not all learners should have access to the same set of courses:

- For example, courses on enterprise solutions used internally should only be available to your organization's employees, but not to external partners, vendors, or customers who have access to your Prime account.
- Courses on future products being developed within your company should only be accessible to the R&D department. Indeed, these courses could contain confidential information that is currently under embargo. This information must not leave the organization before the release of these new products.
- Subscribing to external course libraries provided by third-party vendors comes at a cost. Most of the time, this cost depends on the number of users accessing these online libraries. Therefore, you might want to limit the number of users accessing these third-party courses to those for whom they are most relevant (for instance, courses on leadership for the managers or courses on programming for the developers).
- Some courses might only be relevant in certain parts of the world. This is the case, for example, for courses on local regulations. Only users in the corresponding regions should have access to these courses.

These are just a few of the situations where it is useful to control which learners have access to which courses.

Captivate Prime addresses this problem through the use of catalogs. A **catalog** is a collection of learning items that can contain courses, learning programs, certifications, and job aids. As an administrator, you can create an unlimited number of custom catalogs within your Prime account and decide who has access to which catalog. Controlling who has access to which course is a simple matter of getting the courses into the right catalog(s).

A single course can be part of multiple catalogs and a single catalog can contain an unlimited number of learning items.

Now that you have a high-level understanding of what catalogs are and why you might want to use them, let’s return to Captivate Prime to explore this feature hands-on. Let’s begin by creating catalogs in the next section.
