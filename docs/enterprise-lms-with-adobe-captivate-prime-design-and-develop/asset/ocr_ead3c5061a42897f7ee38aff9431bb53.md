This concludes your overview of the announcements of Adobe Captivate Prime.
Remember that Captivate Prime provides three types of announcements:

+   - **Recommendations** were discussed in *Chapter 13, AI-Powered Recommendations for Learners*. You can use them to manually recommend courses, learning programs, certifications, and job aids to targeted user groups. These manual recommendations are displayed on the learner home page, next to the recommendations that have been generated by the built-in artificial intelligence of Captivate Prime.
+   - **Notifications** are displayed as dialog boxes in the learner experience whenever a learner logs in. They are also available in the notification icon (the bell icon), located in the upper-right corner of the learner experience. In addition to that, Captivate Prime also sends an email message to all the users who have been targeted by the notification when the notification gets published.
+   - **Mastheads** are displayed at the top of the learner experience. What makes them special is the fact that they can be used as call to actions to redirect the learners to another web page.

# Configuring the Messaging System

If you have completed the various exercises that have been provided so far in this book, then the users you have defined in *Chapter 5, Managing Users*, should have received a significant number of email messages from Captivate Prime. In fact, almost every event that takes place in Captivate Prime triggers an email. Managers have received even more messages because in addition to messages about their activities as learners (remember that managers are, first and foremost, learners), they also receive messages about the activities of their team members.

This is an intentional approach to get learners to come back to Captivate Prime regularly, despite the large number of online platforms we all use every day. As such, these emails extend the reach of the Captivate Prime experience and can be considered the beginning of many Prime workflows.

This is the reason why many organizations pay particular attention to both the visual design and the content of these email communications.
