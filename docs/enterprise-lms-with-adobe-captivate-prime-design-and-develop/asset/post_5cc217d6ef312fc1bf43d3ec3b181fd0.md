
## Working With Peer Accounts

Sometimes, a single Captivate Prime account is enough for an organization to manage all of its training offerings. However, it's not uncommon for larger organizations to use several different Captivate Prime accounts. This allows for a great deal of flexibility and makes it possible to integrate Captivate Prime into virtually any type of organization, regardless of its size, complexity, and internal structure:

- Multinational organizations can use multiple Prime accounts for their various branches and regional offices.
- Multiple Prime accounts can be used for the different brands owned by a single corporation.
- Some organizations use multiple Prime accounts to increase the security of their training. Courses containing confidential information are hosted on a separate Prime account, which only accredited learners have access to.
- Etc.

The increased flexibility provided using multiple Prime accounts manifests itself in three distinct ways.

First, there is the content aspect. Each Prime account has a specific content library and its own set of courses, learning programs, certifications, and job aids. As discussed in the External sharing section earlier in this chapter, a Prime account can push content into other Prime accounts using custom catalogs shared externally. This allows, for example, regional offices of a multinational organization to manage their course offerings independently from the main office, while allowing the main office to push globally relevant corporate content into each of its subsidiary Prime accounts.

Second, there is the financial aspect; in other words, who pays for the user seats used in each Prime account? Once again, the great flexibility of Captivate Prime allows you to look at this side of things from several different perspectives:

- Organizations can decide to let each Prime account pay for its own use. This is the default scenario. As far as billing is concerned, each Prime account is a different customer that purchases its own set of user seats directly from Adobe.
- Some organizations prefer to centrally purchase all of the user seats that are needed for all of their Prime accounts. In this case, the cost of Captivate Prime is fully covered by the corporate headquarters, and all user seats are purchased within a single Prime account. The Prime account that purchased the seats can then allocate them to its other Prime accounts using the Peer Accounts feature.
- Of course, a blend of these two models is also possible. A portion of the user seats available in a specific Prime account can be purchased directly from that account, while another portion of the available seats is allocated from another Prime account.

Third, there is the branding aspect. Since each Prime account can be branded, peer accounts are very useful for organizations that need to have a completely different brand image with specific logos and colors for each of their individual Prime accounts. This is especially useful for large companies that have multiple brands.

It is now time to explore the **Peer Accounts** feature, hands-on, using the following steps:

- 1. Log in to your Prime account as an administrator.
- 2. From the administrator home page, click the **Settings** link located in the **Configure** section of the left sidebar.
- 3. Then, click the **Peer Accounts** link located in the **Integrations** section of the left sidebar to open the **Peer Accounts** page of the administrator.

From this page, you can view a list of seats and reports you have shared with other accounts along with a list of seats and reports that you have received from another account:

## Summary

In this chapter, you discovered three important features of Captivate Prime: catalogs, Content Marketplace and peer accounts.

Catalogs allow you to control which learners have access to which learning objects. Remember, you don’t directly control who has access to which course, but you do control who has access to which catalog. So, by adding courses, learning programs, certifications, and job aids to the various catalogs available in your Prime account, you effectively enforce access control on your learning content.

Catalogs can be shared internally or externally. You use internal sharing to determine which learners *defined within your Prime account* have access to what catalog. The learners defined within your Prime account are all the internal and the external learners, as discussed in *Chapter 5, Managing Users*. You use external sharing when you want learners defined in other Prime accounts to access your learning content.

The **Default Catalog** was created at the same time as the Prime account. It contains all of the learning objects that are available in your Prime account and is visible to all of your learners. While you can change the group(s) of users who have access to the **Default Catalog**, you cannot change its contents and you cannot delete it. Therefore, an effective access control policy often involves disabling the **Default Catalog** altogether.

You can also deploy additional course catalogs using the new **Content Marketplace** feature that was added to Captivate Prime with the August 2021 update. Thanks to this new feature, organizations can easily purchase access to additional course catalogs without even leaving the Captivate Prime environment.

On the other end, peer accounts allow you to purchase large numbers of user seats using a single Prime account and allocate some of those seats to other Prime accounts. This feature is used by larger organizations that want to centrally control the cost of their Captivate Prime deployment while using multiple independent Prime accounts for each of their regional branches.

It is easy to confuse externally shared custom catalogs with peer accounts. To differentiate between them, remember that externally shared custom catalogs are intended to make *content* available to other Prime accounts. By using this feature, you are acting on the editorial and instructional design aspects of your course offering.

In contrast, by using peer accounts, you act on the *billing* and *branding* aspects of your Prime deployment. Do you want to buy a lot of seats in one Prime account and then allocate those seats to your other accounts? Or do you prefer to let each of your Prime accounts manage its own costs?

Since these are two different features, you can decide to use either one of them or both at the same time. This makes Captivate Prime so incredibly flexible that it can fit into virtually any organization, regardless of how it is operated internally.

These messages play a critical role in how Captivate Prime operates and how learners engage with the platform. In the next chapter, you'll learn about the announcement and messaging system of Adobe Captivate Prime and customize all of these important messages.
