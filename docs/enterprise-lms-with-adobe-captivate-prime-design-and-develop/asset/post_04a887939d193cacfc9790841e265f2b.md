
## Mapping Skills to Skill Domains

Although a very powerful technology, artificial intelligence needs the input of actual human beings to perform.

In the case of the built-in curation engine of Captivate Prime, the Prime engineering team has defined a handful of skill domains and has trained artificial intelligence in those skill domains. So, by default, the artificial intelligence only knows about these predefined skill domains and has no idea what the custom skills you define on your Prime account refer to.

That's why you must map the skills available on your Prime account to one or more of these predefined skill domains. This is what you will do in this section by performing the following steps:

- 1. Make sure you are logged into your Prime account as an administrator.
- 2. From the administrator home page, click the **Skills** link located in the **Manage** section of the left column.

Creating custom skills was discussed in *Chapter 4, Creating Skills and Courses*. In that chapter, you created the **Cybersecurity** skill, which you will now map to one or more of the predefined skill domains known to Captivate Prime.

> **Note**
> If you have not completed the exercises in *Chapter 4, Creating Skills and Courses*, use one of the skills linked to any of the discussion boards available in your Prime account.

- 3. Click the **Cybersecurity** skill you created back in *Chapter 4, Creating Skills and Courses*, to open the **Edit Skill** dialog.
- 4. In the middle of the dialog, spot the **Skill Domain** option and click the associated **Know More** link.

This action opens a page of the official Captivate Prime user guide containing the current list of skill domains known to Captivate Prime. Browse the list and take note of the skill domains that apply to the skill you clicked on. In this example, you will link your custom **Cybersecurity** skill to the **Computer security** and **Information technology** skill domains.

- 5. Return to the browser window/tab where the **Edit Skill** dialog is visible.
- 6. Type the first few letters of **Computer security** in the **Skill Domain** field. Click the **Computer security** skill domain when it appears in the list.
- 7. Repeat the previous step with the **Information technology** skill domain.

- 8. When the **Edit Skill** dialog looks as follows, click the **Save** button:

![](img/5ceaefa2a9a737206c59f0b54e1a07d0_477_0.png)

Thanks to this process, you told the artificial intelligence powering Captivate Prime’s curation engine that the custom **Cybersecurity** skill you defined in *Chapter 4, Creating Skills and Courses*, is related to the **Computer security** and **Information technology** skill domains that it knows about.

The built-in curation engine of Captivate Prime is now ready to start analyzing the contents that's been uploaded by the learners on the discussion boards that are linked to the **Cybersecurity** skill. But even the most sophisticated artificial intelligence technology is not foolproof. Remember that if automatic curation fails, manual curation by a living, breathing SME is required. Before you give content curation a try, you still need to review the options that allow administrators to manage the list of SMEs for each of the available skills.

## Managing SMEs

Content curation heavily relies on SMEs reviewing the content being shared by learners. In this section, we'll review how you can become an SME and how administrators can manually fine-tune the list of SMEs for each of the available skills.

Remember that when learners participate in social learning activities, they earn points. Earlier in this chapter, you saw how learners receive points upon creating new boards and posting content. Learners also receive points when their content (or comments) are upvoted (liked), when their answer to a question is marked as the correct answer, and so on.

By default, the three users that have accumulated the most points in a given skill are considered SMEs for that skill. Note that each skill has its own list of SMEs and that a single user can serve as an SME for multiple skills.

In addition to these defaults, administrators can also manually designate SMEs by awarding extra SME points to selected learners. Let's review this feature hands-on by performing the following steps:

- 1. Make sure you are logged in as an administrator to your Prime account.
- 2. From the administrator home page, click the Social Learning link located in the Manage section of the left sidebar.
  By default, the Social Learning page of the administrator application opens on the Activity tab.
- 3. Scroll down the Activity tab of the Social Learning page until you see the Skills section.

The Skills section of the Activity tab only shows the skills that are related to one or more of the available discussion boards. In this example, you should see the Cybersecurity skill listed, as shown in the following screenshot:

![](img/5ceaefa2a9a737206c59f0b54e1a07d0_478_0.png)

Figure 12.32 – The Skills section of the Activity tab on the Social Learning page

Take some time to review the metrics available in the **Skills** section of the **Activity** tab. In the preceding screenshot, note that *Learner 01* was automatically designated as an SME for the **Cybersecurity** skill. This is because, in the previous sections of this chapter, *Learner 01* uploaded quite a few documents to the **Cybersecurity in the organization** discussion board. As a result, he/she has accumulated SME points and is now among the three users with the most points for that skill. Note that all of this is done by default; the administrator has not changed any of the available SME-related options yet. However, as an administrator, you can manually modify the list of SMEs.

- 4. Click the **Modify SMEs** link associated with the **Cybersecurity** skill to open the **SMEs for this Skill** dialog.

As shown in the following screenshot, this dialog shows the current list of SMEs for the chosen skill:

![](img/5ceaefa2a9a737206c59f0b54e1a07d0_479_0.png)

Figure 12.33 – The SMEs for this Skill dialog

In the preceding screenshot, *Learner 01* is currently the only SME for the **Cybersecurity** skill, even though that user only has two SME points! This is because the **Cybersecurity in the organization** discussion board is fairly new, with only two learners participating in the discussion so far, so **2 Points** is enough to be considered a top SME for that skill. But 2 social learning points doesn't make someone an expert in Cybersecurity, does it? Let's see what the administrator can do about this

- 5. Click the **Advanced Options** link located in the lower-left corner of the dialog.
- 6. Select the **Enable Minimum SME Points** checkbox and type 50 in the corresponding field.

This means that a minimum of **50** points is required for a learner to become an SME in the **Cybersecurity** skill. This ensures that the learner has some insight and experience in that skill when the system makes him/her an SME.

But now that you have activated the **Enable Minimum SME Points** option, *Learner 01*, with its **2 Points**, can no longer be an SME in that skill, leaving the **Cybersecurity** skill without an SME at all! Let's remedy this situation by manually giving *Learner 01* 100 SME points in the **Cybersecurity** skill. Thanks to this little tweak, the **Cybersecurity** skill will have an SME again, and that SME will have a lot of credibility in the eyes of other learners participating in the corresponding discussion boards.

- 7. Type the first letters of *Learner 01* in the **Type to Search for users** field. Click *Learner 01* when it appears in the list.
- 8. Enter 100 in the **Add Points** field for *Learner 01*. These additional points will be added to the current points, giving *Learner 01* a total of 102 points.
- 9. Click the **Save** button when you're done.

With 102 SME points in the **Cybersecurity** skill, *Learner 01* is now an undisputed expert in Cybersecurity, so the organization can rely on this user for content curation when the AI-powered, built-in curation engine falls short.
