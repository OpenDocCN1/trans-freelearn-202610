# Summary

Zooming out and taking a bird's-eye view of the situation, we can say that courses are what learners enroll in to acquire new skills or update existing skills. For organizations, maintaining a skilled workforce is critical in being successful and profitable.

In this chapter, you learned that Captivate Prime features a full-fledged skills manager that allows organizations to align skills to courses. This allows learners to choose the course they enroll in and track the skills available in the organization. A handful of skills are already available in Prime by default, but you can also use the skills manager to define your own custom skills and assign them to courses.

You also reviewed all the options pertaining to course creation. A course can be considered a list of modules. These can be asynchronous, self-paced modules available in the Content Library, synchronous instructor-led live or virtual classes, as well as various types of activity modules such as external URLs, file submissions, and checklists. Prework and Checkout modules, as well as Job Aids and other resources, can also be added to courses. All these options make it possible to implement a wide range of instructional design strategies using Adobe Captivate Prime.

At this point, though, your LMS still lacks one final ingredient before learning can finally occur. This last ingredient is the users that will use the platform to take courses, teach courses, and administer the system. Managing users is what you will learn about in the next chapter.
