## Editing a published course

There are several reasons why it might be useful to edit a published course. For example, new regulations, policy changes, new software versions, and so on require that courses are updated. Editing a published course is very similar to creating a course. Let's take a look:

- 1. From the Author home page, click the **courses** link in the **Learning** section of the left sidebar to access the Course Catalog.
- 2. Hover over your course card and click the **View Course** link. This will take you back to the course **Overview** page, where *all fields are disabled by default*.
- 3. Click the **Edit** button in the top-right corner of the page to unlock the form.
- 4. Make the necessary changes.
- 5. When you're ready, click the **Republish** button to see your changes.

By default, republishing a course sends an email to all learners currently enrolled in the course to notify them about the update. In *Chapter 15, Working With Messages and Announcements*, you will learn how to enable/disable and customize this and other messages available in Prime.

Also, be aware that updating courses in such a way does *not* change the completion and success criteria that have already been achieved by currently enrolled learners.

## Retiring and deleting courses

From time to time, it may be necessary to delete a course altogether. This can happen when one of your products reaches its end of life, when you decide to no longer work with a given provider, when a given software solution has been abandoned, when a new version of a course is created from scratch, and so on.

Similar to modules, skills, and other types of content available in Prime, you cannot directly *delete* a course. You must *retire* it first; only then can it be deleted:

- A *published* course accepts new enrollments.
- When the course is *retired*, it is removed from the Course Catalog and does not accept any new enrollments. Currently, enrolled users continue to view the course normally and are still able to review modules to complete the course. Also, a *retired* course can still be republished, in which case it returns to the Course Catalog and starts accepting new enrollments again.
- When a course is *deleted*, it is completely removed from your Prime account. All the data about the course is lost and learners lose access to it. That's why you need to proceed with great care when deleting a course. Note that a course must be in the *retired* state before it can be deleted.

Perform the following steps to retire and then delete a course:

- 1. From the Author home page, click the **Courses** link in the **Learning** section of the left sidebar to access the Course Catalog.
- 2. Hover over the course card of the course you want to retire.
- 3. Click the **Retire** link. Take some time to read the message and click the **Yes** button to confirm that this is what you want to do.
- 4. Click the **Retired** tab located above the list of courses in the Course Catalog.
- 5. Hover over the course card of the course you want to republish/delete.
- 6. Click either the **Publish Course** or **Delete Course** link.

In this example, make sure you do *not* delete any course. Just click the **Publish Course** link to reestablish the course in the course catalog.

In this section, you learned how to retire, republish, and delete courses. It is not uncommon for a course to remain several months or even years in the *Retired* state to allow learners to complete it before it is permanently deleted. Some organizations also decide to keep their old courses in the *Retired* state without ever deleting them to keep them in their archives.

## 5 Managing Users

After reading the previous chapter, there is learning content hosted on your Prime account. This content is ready to be delivered to your learners. Except... you don't have any learners yet! In this chapter, you will review the tools and features of Captivate Prime that allow you to manage the users that will be granted access to your Prime account.

When it comes to user management, there are two important aspects to keep in mind: **authentication** and **authorization**. *Authentication* answers the question, "Who are you?", while *authorization* answers the question, "What can you do?"

For authentication, you can, of course, define users directly within Captivate Prime. But as the enterprise LMS of choice, Captivate Prime understands that users may already be defined in a corporate directory, an **Enterprise Resource Planning (ERP)** or a **Customer Relationship Management (CRM)** system, and so on. That's why Captivate Prime can retrieve user data from virtually any third-party system, either through a dedicated connector or by automatically importing CSV files via an FTP connection.

You can also decide to integrate Captivate Prime within your **single sign-on (SSO)** infrastructure, providing that your organization has a SAML 2.0-compliant SSO provider. This allows your users to authenticate only once and gain access to multiple enterprise applications, including Captivate Prime.

When it comes to authorization, Captivate Prime adopts a role-based approach. You have already visited the main default roles of the system (*Administrator, Author, Integration Admin, and Learner*), but you can also define custom roles to fit the specific needs of your organization.
