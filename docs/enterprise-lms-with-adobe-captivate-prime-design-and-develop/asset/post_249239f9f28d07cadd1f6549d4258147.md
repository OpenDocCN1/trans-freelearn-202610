## Exploring the Instructor Role

In the previous chapter, you achieved an important milestone: learners are now taking courses on your Prime account. While some of these courses involve only self-paced asynchronous activities that can be managed entirely by the system, other types of activities require human intervention.

First of all, there are the (face-to-face or virtual) instructor-led classes. Obviously, you need an instructor to conduct them. But that's not all. Instructor-led classes also require attendance and waitlist management, as well as sending reminders and additional resources (such as prep work, maps, access badges, and so on) to participants.

There are also the file submission activities that require submitted files to be reviewed by an instructor, and the checklist activities that also require human intervention to be marked as completed and successful.

The primary task of the instructor role is to follow up on these activities. As discussed in Chapter 5, User Management, it is necessary for an administrator to assign the Instructor role to a user profile for that user to become an instructor. In addition, the person must also be designated as an instructor for an instructor-led (face-to-face or virtual) class, a file submission activity, or a checklist activity. Common examples of users being assigned the instructor role include in-house staff trainers, external vendors that train on specific content, or an instructor providing training that can only be administered and graded physically (CPR certification, role-play training, and so on).

In this chapter, you will log in to your Prime account as an instructor and review the tools and features that enable instructors to carry out their assigned tasks. To achieve this goal, this chapter takes you through the following sections:

- Managing instructor-led (face-to-face and virtual) classes
- Reviewing file submission assignments
- Filling out checklist activities

The role of the instructor is very important to the day-to-day operations of your Captivate Prime account and to the quality of the learner experience, especially in an era where face-to-face and distance learning are bound to become increasingly blended. With Captivate Prime, trainers have all the tools they need to build the blended courses that are becoming increasingly popular in more and more organizations. So, let's dive right into the instructor role!

## Technical requirements

In order to perform the exercises described in this chapter, you need to meet the following requirements:

- You must have instructor access to a working Captivate Prime account, which can be a trial account.
- The instructor account must be defined as the instructor for at least one (live or virtual) class activity, one file submission activity, and one checklist activity.
- Students must be registered for the instructor-led class and you must have at least one additional student on the waitlist.
- At least one file should have been submitted for the file submission activity.

If you have completed the exercises in the previous chapters, you are sure to meet the above requirements! If not, you can use your own course content and users to complete the exercises in this chapter. However, the instructions are based on the exercises from the previous chapters, so feel free to adapt them to your personal situation.

## Managing instructor-led Face-to-face or Virtual Classes

When self-paced modules, such as video files, PDF documents, SCORM packages, and so on, are used in a Prime course, they require very little further human intervention. Learners can review these modules at their own convenience, quizzes are graded automatically, and their results are automatically reported back to Prime, with success and completion criteria being evaluated by the system. All in all, Captivate Prime is able to deliver and track these course modules almost entirely on its own.

Instructor-led courses, on the other hand, require a lot more logistics. Whether face-to-face or virtual, these types of activities are known as synchronous activities. This means that the instructor and the learners are present at the same time in a classroom or lecture hall, or connected to the same conference call or virtual classroom environment. Obviously, this raises scheduling concerns, but that's not all:

- Typical classrooms and lecture halls have limited seating. If enrollment is too high, you must be ready to manage a waitlist, move the session to a larger venue, or create another instance of the session to accommodate everyone. And of course, your decision must be communicated to learners in a professional and timely fashion. Such a follow-up is an integral part of a great learning experience.
- A registered student may be unable to attend and decide to withdraw. In such cases, a seat is made available for the next person on the waitlist who must be duly contacted and registered for the session.
- The classroom or lecture hall may require secure access, so you need to ensure that each learner has access to the facility on the day of the session. This may require sending participants their personal access badges, a site map, access plan, security procedure, and so on. If the class is virtual, you must send the participants the link to the class, ensure they meet the technical requirements, that they have installed the required software, and so on.
- Prerequisite work may be required before the session begins. This means that someone must collect the assignments, send reminders to students who do not turn in their prework on time, assess and grade the prework submitted by learners, and so on.
- On class day, attendance must be properly taken and reported back to the LMS.
- Attending a class may not be sufficient to make it a success. In many cases, successfully attending a class means participating in the activities, answering the instructor's questions, taking part in discussions, turning in an assignment at the end of the day, and so on. The synchronous nature of this type of activity makes it impossible for a computer system to gauge the participation of each learner. This is another instance where human intervention remains essential.
- Reminders must be sent to students in advance, so everyone is on the same page when the date of the class finally arrives, and so on.

In Captivate Prime, these are the main tasks of the Instructor role. They must be completed in a timely and professional manner, as they are essential to the overall learner experience. This is what you will review in this section.

## Reviewing session details

When an instructor is designated for a class, the first thing they probably want to look at is the details of the class, such as the location, the topic, the number of attendees already registered, and so on. In Captivate Prime, these details are only one click away from the instructor home page. Let's review this by following these steps:

1. Make sure you are logged in as an instructor and currently located on the instructor home page.
2. Click any of your upcoming sessions to review the session details.
