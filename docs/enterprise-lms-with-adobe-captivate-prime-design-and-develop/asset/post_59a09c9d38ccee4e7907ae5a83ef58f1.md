## Creating custom groups

In this section, you will create the *Advisory Board* group. This group comprises the CEO, some managers, and some employees. It does not correspond to any department, location, role, hierarchical relationship, and so on in the organization and therefore cannot be automatically generated by Captivate Prime.

Perform the following steps to create a new custom user group:

- 1. From the **User Groups** page, click the **Add** button located in the top-right corner of the screen. This will bring up the **Add User Group** form.

- 3. As a best practice, take the necessary time to supply a short **Description** for your new group.

  Type the name of a user in the **Add Learners – Name** field and select the desired user when it appears in the list. Notice that you can also select other groups rather than individual users. This is how you can create nested groups.
  If you're using an **Email ID**, you must supply a comma-separated list of email addresses of existing users.

## Managing user roles

Within a platform such as Captivate Prime, authentication and authorization are the two fundamental aspects of user management. So far in this chapter, we have only focused on authentication. Authentication answers the who are you? question, and you have seen how internal and external users, as well as login methods, allow Captivate Prime to answer that question.

In this section, you will focus on the authorization aspect of user management. Authorization answers the what can you do? question. Indeed, just because you can authenticate on a platform does not mean that you are allowed to use all its features. For example, learners cannot create courses, authors cannot delete users, and so on. Only the administrator is allowed to access every corner of the platform.

In Captivate Prime, authorization is role-based. This means you need to create roles and define what each role can do on the platform. Then, you need to assign one or more roles to users.

There are five basic roles already defined in Captivate Prime: **Learner**, **Author**, **Instructor**, **Integration Admin**, and **Administrator**. Let's take a look at them:

- **The Learner** role is the default role that's assigned to each new user (internal or external). A learner can browse through the Course Catalog, enroll in courses, take courses, and more.
- **Authors** are instructional designers and content creators. They can be internal or external users, and they have access to the Content Library to upload instructional content and create new course modules. They can also assemble these modules into courses and learning programs.
- **Instructors** are the teachers that conduct the live and virtual class activities. They also review the assignments that have been uploaded by learners through the file submission activities and fill in the checklists defined in the checklist activities. All these activities were discussed in *Chapter 4, Creating Skills and Courses*.
- **Integration Admins** are typically IT professionals or developers, whose job is to configure the connectors that integrate Captivate Prime with other systems in your organization and to develop applications that use Captivate Prime as a backend. Note that external users cannot be made Integration Admins.
- Finally, **Administrators** are in charge of the day-to-day management of your Prime account. They can add and remove users, assign roles to users, create skills, enroll users in courses, and so on. They are also in charge of the general setup of Captivate Prime and the daily operation of the platform.

> **Instructors versus authors**
> Coming from the education sector, it took me a while to understand the rationale for separating the roles of *Instructor* and *Author*. After all, in most schools and universities, the teacher is both the author and the instructor of his/her courses. But that's not necessarily the case in the corporate world, where a team of instructional designers may be responsible for creating courses that are then delivered by a pool of instructors. This allows the same course to be delivered in many different locations (for example, different branches) and in different languages, a pattern frequently found in large, multinational organizations. This is another example where it appears that Captivate Prime is designed as an enterprise LMS.

These roles are considered *explicit* roles because they must be explicitly assigned to users by an administrator for Captivate Prime to authorize user access to the corresponding features.
