# Managing instructor-led Face-to-face or Virtual Classes

When self-paced modules, such as video files, PDF documents, SCORM packages, and so on, are used in a Prime course, they require very little further human intervention. Learners can review these modules at their own convenience, quizzes are graded automatically, and their results are automatically reported back to Prime, with success and completion criteria being evaluated by the system. All in all, Captivate Prime is able to deliver and track these course modules almost entirely on its own.

Instructor-led courses, on the other hand, require a lot more logistics. Whether face-to-face or virtual, these types of activities are known as synchronous activities. This means that the instructor and the learners are present at the same time in a classroom or lecture hall, or connected to the same conference call or virtual classroom environment. Obviously, this raises scheduling concerns, but that's not all:

+   - Typical classrooms and lecture halls have limited seating. If enrollment is too high, you must be ready to manage a waitlist, move the session to a larger venue, or create another instance of the session to accommodate everyone. And of course, your decision must be communicated to learners in a professional and timely fashion. Such a follow-up is an integral part of a great learning experience.
+   - A registered student may be unable to attend and decide to withdraw. In such cases, a seat is made available for the next person on the waitlist who must be duly contacted and registered for the session.
+   - The classroom or lecture hall may require secure access, so you need to ensure that each learner has access to the facility on the day of the session. This may require sending participants their personal access badges, a site map, access plan, security procedure, and so on. If the class is virtual, you must send the participants the link to the class, ensure they meet the technical requirements, that they have installed the required software, and so on.
+   - Prerequisite work may be required before the session begins. This means that someone must collect the assignments, send reminders to students who do not turn in their prework on time, assess and grade the prework submitted by learners, and so on.
+   - On class day, attendance must be properly taken and reported back to the LMS.
+   - Attending a class may not be sufficient to make it a success. In many cases, successfully attending a class means participating in the activities, answering the instructor's questions, taking part in discussions, turning in an assignment at the end of the day, and so on. The synchronous nature of this type of activity makes it impossible for a computer system to gauge the participation of each learner. This is another instance where human intervention remains essential.
