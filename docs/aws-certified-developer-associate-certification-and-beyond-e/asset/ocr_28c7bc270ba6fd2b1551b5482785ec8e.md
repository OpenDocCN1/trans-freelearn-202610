At a high level, EventBridge can monitor when events occur in your AWS account and then respond to them. EventBridge uses the concept of an event bus, which is essentially a stream of events that occur from any AWS service and resources in your account. This event bus monitors state change, and if a change occurs, it can trigger an event that calls a target. A target can be another supported AWS service, such as a Lambda function or a notification to an SNS topic.

For example, EventBridge can trigger a notification to an SNS topic when one of your users stops a particular EC2 instance, as illustrated in Figure 16.8. You may need to be notified of such events, such as for auditing purposes.

Using the Cron Job tool, you can also configure EventBridge to schedule events to be triggered at a specific time. For example, you may wish to trigger an event that calls a Lambda function to automatically stop your testing servers at 6 PM at the end of a working day. You may also want to restart those instances again at 9 AM every day and repeat this operation automatically every weekday. In addition, you could also configure events to occur following a rate expression, for example, to trigger an action every 10 minutes.

Amazon CloudWatch can provide valuable insights into the operational health of your AWS account and its resources. It offers extensive capabilities for logging, monitoring, and remediation action as necessary.

# Introduction to AWS CloudTrail

AWS CloudTrail records events in your AWS account, providing a detailed documentary of user actions, roles, or AWS services. Each event captured by CloudTrail provides comprehensive information that can be used for security analysis, compliance auditing, and operational troubleshooting.
