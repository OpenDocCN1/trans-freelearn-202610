While Multi-AZ and read replicas can help address concerns related to the source database failing, you must remember that they cannot necessarily protect against data corruption. If data gets corrupted on the source database instance, then due to the nature of replication, this corrupted data will be replicated to the Multi-AZ standby as well as any read replicas. In this scenario, you should revert to backups that were hopefully created before the corruption occurred. You can configure Amazon RDS for both automatic and manual backups and You will learn more about this next.

# Automatic and Manual Backups

Amazon RDS offers two backup options for your database instances. These are manual snapshots and automatic backups. As a managed service, you only need to enable automatic backups or start a manual snapshot and AWS will take care of the rest in terms of taking the snapshots and storing them. All backed-up data is stored in Amazon S3 within AWS-managed folders, so you do not have direct access to the S3 buckets that contain your data.

Let’s look at each backup option and how they work within the Amazon RDS platform.

# Manual Snapshots

You can take manual snapshots of your database instance at any point in time. When you take a backup, you are taking a snapshot of the entire database instance and so this will include all databases created on that instance.

You can take manual snapshots as frequently as you like, which means the more often you take a backup, the lower your **recovery point objective (RPO)**. An RPO refers to the amount of acceptable data loss measured in time. So, for example, if you take a backup once every 24 hours then your RPO is 1 day and you are willing to accept a worst-case scenario of losing a day’s worth of data.

Another factor to consider is that if you take a backup of a single DB instance, there will be a brief I/O suspension that could last between a few seconds and a few minutes depending on the size of the database. This is normal behavior and ideally, you should take your snapshot outside of normal business hours. If you wish to take more frequent backups during business hours, consider configuring your database with Multi-AZ. In this configuration, the backups are taken from the standby copy of the database and so any I/O suspension is not noticed by the end user of the database.

> **Note**
> 
> This capability of avoiding brief I/O suspensions by using a Multi-AZ configuration where backups are taken from the standby instance is not available for Microsoft SQL Server. Customers will experience a brief I/O suspension during backups even with Multi-AZ configurations on this engine type.
