> **Best practice**
> When granting access to services, you must follow the POLP as recommended by AWS. This ensures that those identities are provided with only the necessary access needed to carry out approved tasks and perform their job functions.

As depicted in the following diagram, the root user has unrestricted access. However, your IAM users do not have any permissions when you first create them. Although these IAM users can log in to the AWS account, they will not be able to access any services or create resources. IAM users require permissions that are defined by policies you can attach to those users (or the group that the user belongs to). These permissions define what actions those users can perform in your account.

# IAM Groups

IAM groups enable you to collate individual IAM users who share common job functions. This allows you to assign permissions to the group as a whole and the users that members of the group, will inherit those permissions. Assigning permissions to groups is much easier from a management perspective as it avoids creating and attaching multiple policies to every user directly. This makes managing permission assignments and auditing much more straightforward.
