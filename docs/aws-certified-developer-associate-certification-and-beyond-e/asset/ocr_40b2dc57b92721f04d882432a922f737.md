Ultimately, provisioning capacity requires understanding the application’s requirements. Next, you will look at some additional concepts on how DynamoDB manages provisioned capacity.

DynamoDB is automatically enabled for **adaptive capacity**. This feature enables DynamoDB to run workloads that are unpredictable in nature and helps to minimize throttling exceptions. The feature also offers a better mechanism to handle *hot partitions*. A hot partition is one that receives higher volumes of read and write requests compared with other partitions. DynamoDB uses its adaptive capacity to handle uneven access patterns by automatically increasing throughput for partitions that receive more traffic than others. Figure 6.17 – DynamoDB adaptive capacity

In the preceding diagram, you have a table provisioned with 400 WCUs evenly spread across four partitions. Let’s assume that partition 4 has some very popular data and becomes a hot partition. The total capacity required for partition 4 due to demand is 150 WCU/sec. With this huge increase in write capacity, eventually, you would expect traffic to be throttled as it exceeds 100 WUC/sec. However, with adaptive capacity, DynamoDB will increase partitions 4’s capacity drawing on the available capacity from the other partitions to help handle the higher workload of 150 WCU/sec without being throttled.

Another feature is known as **burst capacity**. Here, DynamoDB reserves a portion of unused capacity (a maximum of 300 seconds of unused read and write capacity) for handling any occasional usage spikes as they occur.
