As the magnetic field of the stator rotates, its poles attract the opposite poles of the rotor and this creates a turning force that causes the rotor to rotate at the same speed as that of the rotating magnetic field (synchronous speed). The term synchronous motor means that the speed of the rotor of the motor is the same as the speed of the rotating magnetic field of the stator.

The synchronous speed of an AC motor is the speed of the rotating magnetic field. It is determined by the frequency of the voltage source and the number of poles, as shown in the following formula:

Synchronous speed = 120f/P

Where f = frequency in Hz and P = number of poles.

Synchronous motors can be used for applying constant speed from no load to full load. They are good for use in high-precision applications in industry and are also well suited for robotic actuators.
