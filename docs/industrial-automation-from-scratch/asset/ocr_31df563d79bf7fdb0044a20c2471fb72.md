# Understanding analog signals

Unlike digital signals, which can only be in two states (0 or 1), analog signals have a continuous range of values from the minimum to the maximum. They can present what’s between 0 (minimum, for example, 0 V or 4 mA) and 1 (maximum, for example, 5 V, 10 V or 20 mA). When a voltage varies from 0 V to 10 V, or when a current varies from 4 mA to 20 mA, it is called an analog signal. Examples of sensors and transmitters that produce an analog signal output are temperature transmitters, pressure transmitters, and flow transmitters. Before a PLC can process analog values, it must be converted into digital information first – this is done by the Analog-to-Digital Converter (ADC) in the analog module of a PLC. One important feature of an analog module is its resolution in bits.
