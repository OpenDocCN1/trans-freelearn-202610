At first glance, these might seem like stringent limitations. But consider the broader picture: imagine a scenario where a subscriber has built a local class based on your global interface. If you were to alter or delete that interface, it would directly impact and potentially break the subscriber’s local customizations. The rigidity of global is, in essence, a protective measure, ensuring stability and reliability for all parties involved.

In light of these considerations, treat the global keyword with the utmost respect. When planning for DI, ensure your interfaces and classes are robust and well thought out before marking them as global. It’s not just about flexibility; it’s a promise of consistency and reliability to your subscribers.
