By staying alert to these signs, you’ll be better equipped to identify and tackle technical debt proactively, maintaining a healthy development process for your product.

# The cost of technical debt

While technical debt may allow companies to achieve short-term goals more rapidly, it’s crucial to understand that this comes with a cost. Like financial debt, technical debt incurs interest over time in the form of additional resources needed for maintenance, refactoring, and troubleshooting. Moreover, the longer the debt remains unpaid, the more interest it accrues, making it progressively more challenging and costly to repay.

Beyond the direct costs in time and resources, technical debt can also have indirect costs. It can lower the overall quality of the product, leading to a poor user experience. This can, in turn, negatively impact customer satisfaction, which could harm your brand’s reputation and potentially lead to loss of business.

Technical debt can also make your code base more complex and harder to understand, which increases the risk of introducing new bugs and makes onboarding new developers more challenging.
