**Pix2Pix – Paired Image Translation:** Pix2Pix, proposed by Phillip Isola et al. (2017), assumes the availability of *paired* training samples $(x,y)$, where each input image has a corresponding ground-truth target image. The generator learns $G:x\to y$, while the discriminator distinguishes real image pairs $(x,y)$ from fake pairs $(x,G(x))$. The objective combines an adversarial loss with an $L_1$ reconstruction loss:

$$L_{\mathrm{Pix2Pix}}=L_{\mathrm{GAN}}(G,D)+\lambda L_1(G),$$

where $L_1(G)=\mathbb{E}_{x,y}[\|y-G(x)\|_1]$. The adversarial term encourages realism, whereas the reconstruction term preserves correspondence with the target image.

**CycleGAN – Unpaired Image Translation:** Obtaining paired datasets is often expensive or impossible. CycleGAN, introduced by Jun-Yan Zhu et al. (2017), addresses this limitation by learning from two unpaired image collections.

Consider two domains: $X=$ Horses, $Y=$ Zebras. CycleGAN learns two generators: $G:X\to Y$, $F:Y\to X$, along with two discriminators $D_X$ and $D_Y$.

The key innovation is the **cycle-consistency constraint**:

$$F(G(x))\approx x,\quad G(F(y))\approx y$$

This yields the cycle-consistency loss:

$$L_{\mathrm{cyc}}=\mathbb{E}_x[\|F(G(x))-x\|_1]+\mathbb{E}_y[\|G(F(y))-y\|_1]$$

The complete objective is:

$$L=L_{\mathrm{GAN}}(G,D_Y)+L_{\mathrm{GAN}}(F,D_X)+\lambda L_{\mathrm{cyc}}$$

The cycle constraint prevents arbitrary mappings and helps preserve content while changing appearance.

> **Note**
> 
> In *paired* settings, each input image is aligned with a ground-truth target (e.g., image-to-mask mapping), whereas in *unpaired* settings the two domains are independent and unaligned (e.g., separate collections of horse and zebra images without one-to-one correspondence).
