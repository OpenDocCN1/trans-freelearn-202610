# Scale-space blob detection with LoG, DoG, and DoH (scikit-image)

In image processing, space refers to the 2D domain of image coordinates, typically representing pixel intensities. Scale, on the other hand, refers to the level of detail at which an image is analyzed. At fine scales (small values of σ, the standard deviation of a Gaussian filter), image features like small edges and textures are preserved. At coarse scales (larger σ), fine details are suppressed, and only larger structures are retained. This multi-scale representation of an image is referred to as the scale-space.

# Why use scale space?

Objects in real-world scenes can appear at various sizes depending on distance, perspective, or zoom level. Traditional feature detectors operating at a single scale may fail to identify objects that do not match the assumed size. Scale-space theory solves this by analyzing the image across multiple scales, ensuring that features of all sizes can be detected. One of the key applications of scale-space is blob detection, where we aim to identify regions in the image that differ in properties (e.g., intensity/brightness or texture) compared to surrounding areas. These blobs can vary in size, making a multi-scale approach essential. The filters used, LoG, DoG, and Determinant of Hessian (DoH), are grounded in multiscale analysis and differential geometry.
