The multi-Otsu threshold is a thresholding algorithm that is used to separate the pixels of an input image into several different classes, each one obtained according to the intensity of the gray levels within the image. It is a **simple yet powerful** unsupervised segmentation method, with a strong interpretability grounded in statistical variance. It can be considered as a bridge between classical image processing and modern learning-based segmentation.

Multi-Otsu assumes **intensity separability**. It works best when the histogram has distinct peaks and the noise is low. It is sensitive to illumination variation and overlapping intensity distributions.

> **Note**
> 
> Despite the availability of more advanced segmentation techniques, thresholding remains widely used due to its simplicity, computational efficiency, and strong performance in scenarios with clear intensity separation, such as biomedical imaging and document analysis, where it often serves as a fast and reliable baseline.

# ## Edge, region, and topographic segmentation

Segmentation techniques can broadly be categorized into **edge-based**, **region-based**, and **topographic (morphological)** approaches. While edge-based methods rely on detecting intensity discontinuities, region-based methods exploit homogeneity, and topographic methods interpret the image as a geometric surface. This section focuses on **Watershed segmentation** as a unifying topographic method, followed by a practical pipeline combining region and morphology for object counting.

# ## Watershed segmentation

Watershed segmentation is a powerful morphological approach rooted in **mathematical morphology** (pioneered by Jean Serra and Georges Matheron). It interprets an image as a **topographic surface**, where pixel intensities → elevation, dark regions → valleys (basins), and bright regions → mountains (peaks).
