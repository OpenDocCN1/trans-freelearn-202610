
As α varies from 0 to 1, the generated image smoothly transitions between two Fashion-MNIST categories through interpolation of the class-conditioning vector, demonstrating the continuity of the learned conditional latent space.

### Within-Class Latent Traversal:
Unlike *cross-class* traversal, which transitions between different semantic categories (e.g., “sneaker” to “ankle boot” in the last example), *within-class* traversal keeps the class label fixed (e.g., 7 for Sneaker in the next code snippet) and, by *interpolating* between *latent vectors*, the CVAE generates smooth variations of the same object category, demonstrating that the learned latent space captures continuous intra-class attributes such as *shape* and *style*:

```
x1 = img_sneaker.view(1,-1).to(device)
x2 = img_bag.view(1,-1).to(device)
y1 = torch.tensor([7], device=device) # Sneaker
y2 = torch.tensor([8], device=device) # Bag
mu1,_ = model.encode(x1, y1)
mu2,_ = model.encode(x2, y2)

for i,a in enumerate(np.linspace(0,1,15)):
    z = (1-a)*mu1 + a*mu2
    out = model.decode(z, y1)
```

### CVAE versus cGAN:
Compared with cGANs, CVAEs provide a smoother and more interpretable latent space, support meaningful interpolation, and train more reliably because they avoid adversarial optimization. However, cGANs generally produce sharper and more realistic images. Consequently, CVAEs are often preferred when latent-space analysis, representation learning, and controllable generation are more important than photorealism. Also, cGAN latent representations tend to form sharper class-separated clusters, while CVAE latent representations are more overlapped because the KL-divergence regularization encourages all classes to share a common continuous latent manifold (compare 2D-tSNE manifolds).

Overall, CVAEs are preferable when stability, interpretability, and structured latent representations are the primary goals, particularly for tasks such as feature analysis, controlled interpolation, and downstream representation learning, whereas cGANs are better suited for applications where visual fidelity and perceptual sharpness are critical, such as high-quality image synthesis and realistic data augmentation.

## Pix2Pix and CycleGAN for image-to-image translation

Image-to-image translation aims to learn a mapping between two visual domains, making it a practical tool for reducing manual effort and enabling scalable cross-domain visual synthesis in real-world applications. Given an input image *x* from one domain, the objective is to generate a corresponding image *y* in another domain while preserving the underlying scene structure. Examples include sketch-to-photo, satellite-to-map, summer-to-winter, and horse-to-zebra translation. Pix2Pix and CycleGAN are two landmark GAN-based image translation frameworks. While both learn mappings between image domains, they differ fundamentally in the supervision available during training.

### Pix2Pix – Paired Image Translation:
Pix2Pix, proposed by Phillip Isola et al. (2017), assumes the availability of *paired* training samples $(x,y)$, where each input image has a corresponding ground-truth target image. The generator learns $G:x\to y$, while the discriminator distinguishes real image pairs $(x,y)$ from fake pairs $(x,G(x))$. The objective combines an adversarial loss with an $L_1$ reconstruction loss:

$$L_{\mathrm{Pix2Pix}}=L_{\mathrm{GAN}}(G,D)+\lambda L_1(G),$$

where $L_1(G)=\mathbb{E}_{x,y}[\|y-G(x)\|_1]$. The adversarial term encourages realism, whereas the reconstruction term preserves correspondence with the target image.

### CycleGAN – Unpaired Image Translation:
Obtaining paired datasets is often expensive or impossible. CycleGAN, introduced by Jun-Yan Zhu et al. (2017), addresses this limitation by learning from two unpaired image collections.

Consider two domains: $X=$ Horses, $Y=$ Zebras. CycleGAN learns two generators: $G:X\to Y$, $F:Y\to X$, along with two discriminators $D_X$ and $D_Y$.

The key innovation is the **cycle-consistency constraint**:

$$F(G(x))\approx x,\quad G(F(y))\approx y$$

This yields the cycle-consistency loss:

$$L_{\mathrm{cyc}}=\mathbb{E}_x[\|F(G(x))-x\|_1]+\mathbb{E}_y[\|G(F(y))-y\|_1]$$

The complete objective is:

$$L=L_{\mathrm{GAN}}(G,D_Y)+L_{\mathrm{GAN}}(F,D_X)+\lambda L_{\mathrm{cyc}}$$

The cycle constraint prevents arbitrary mappings and helps preserve content while changing appearance.

> **Note**
> 
> In *paired* settings, each input image is aligned with a ground-truth target (e.g., image-to-mask mapping), whereas in *unpaired* settings the two domains are independent and unaligned (e.g., separate collections of horse and zebra images without one-to-one correspondence).
