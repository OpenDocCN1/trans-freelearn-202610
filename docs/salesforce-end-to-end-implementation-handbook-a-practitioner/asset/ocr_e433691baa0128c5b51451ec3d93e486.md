The tricky part is often identifying the root cause and, depending on which deployment (SIT, UAT, or production), the issue has different levels of criticality. Ideally, you identify the issue in SIT (either as part of your development sprints or in a dedicated SIT after development), in which case you can proceed as described in the beginning. Similarly, if you are deploying to your UAT sandbox (or executing UAT). Here, however, there is more pressure as users are typically allocated and planned to carry out UAT, and later, training. This means you need to quickly understand how long a fix will take, and assess your need to change your plan. If you’re deploying to production, you need to assess whether the time to fix impacts your overall deployment and data migration plan – and ultimately, your go-live date. |
