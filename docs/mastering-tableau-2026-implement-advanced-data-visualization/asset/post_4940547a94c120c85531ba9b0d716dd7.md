## Multi-row Calculations

Let me share a feature that has quickly become one of my favorites in recent releases. Starting with Tableau Prep 2023.2 (and enhanced through 2024), you can now perform multi-row calculations directly in your Prep flow. Think of it as bringing table calculations, like moving averages, differences from previous rows, and percent differences, out of Tableau Desktop and into your data preparation layer.

In Tableau Desktop, table calculations are computed after the query returns. This works fine for small datasets, but with larger data, you might notice:

- Dashboards load more slowly because the calculation happens in the view
- Filters can change how your calculation behaves in unexpected ways
- You can’t use the result in subsequent calculations without creating complex nested table calcs

By moving these calculations to Prep, you get three wonderful benefits:

- **Faster dashboards:** The heavy lifting happens before your data ever reaches Tableau Desktop
- **Predictable results:** Filters won't alter how your calculation is computed
- **Reusable outputs:** The result becomes just another field you can use in further calculations

Let's take a look at how to use multi-row calculations.

In any Clean step, select a field, click the More options menu (the three dots), and you'll find three new choices:

- **Difference from:** Calculates the difference between current row and another value
- **Percent difference from:** Same as above but as a percentage
- **Moving calculation:** Returns sum or average within a flexible set of rows

![](img/66cf6b89349fda3581a4f196cb3c98a4_119_0.png)

Figure 3.31: Multi-row Calculation

Let's walk through an example of a 3-month moving average of sales by region.

Using the Superstore data that's become our faithful companion:

1. Add a Clean step after your aggregated sales data (click that + and select Clean Step)
2. Find the Sales field, click the three dots, and select Moving Calculation
3. A configuration panel will appear. Here's where the magic happens, select the following for each section:
   - **Group by:** Select Region (this ensures each region's months are calculated separately)
   - **Order by:** Choose Year of Sale, then Order Month (both ascending)
   - Result type: Change this to Moving AVG (instead of the default Moving SUM)
   - Previous values: Enter 2 (to include current month plus two previous months)
4. Give your new field a descriptive name, something like "3 Month Moving Average of Sales for each Region"
5. Now, when you publish this data source, your dashboard consumers can filter by month to their liking without worrying about the moving average recalculating incorrectly. It’s already baked into the data.

> **Pro tip:** If your timeline has gaps (missing months), consider using a **New Rows** step to scaffold your dates before applying moving calculations. This ensures your averages are truly representative.

We’ll finish this section here and look back at a graph consisting of only 9 datapoints, which we created by aggregating the almost 64,000 rows of reviewer_id data by the year that the host started offering the listing. The graph tells a story; 64,000 individual rows of data don’t. That is why it is so important to always reduce your data and aggregate it if possible. In the next section, we will talk about another important feature: pivoting.

## Pivoting

Do you recognize a situation where you drag and drop your fields onto your Tableau Desktop row and column shelves but somehow the visualization doesn’t do what you want? Chances are that you have to turn your data, or better, pivot it. For our pivot example, we will make use of another dataset. You can find it at https://github.com/PacktPublishing/Mastering-Tableau-2026-/blob/main/Chapter03/Excel_Pivot.xlsx. The dataset is very simple and looks like this:
