
This story is terrifying because it could happen to any of us. We've all had that moment of panic after clicking "Delete" on the wrong file. In the past, recovery meant begging IT to restore from backup, if they had one, and if they could find the right version.

Starting with the 2025.1 release, Tableau Cloud and Tableau Server include a Recycle Bin that works just like the one on your computer. When you delete a workbook, data source, or project, it's not gone forever. It moves to the Recycle Bin, where it stays for up to 30 days (your admin can configure the exact retention period). You find your deleted item, click Restore, and it's back exactly where it belongs.

This is governance that protects you personally, not just the organization. Yes, it helps companies prevent data loss. But more importantly, it saves you from career-limiting mistakes and stress-filled evenings.

A few important details:

- You can only restore items you had permission to delete in the first place
- The Recycle Bin shows who deleted what and when
- Items are permanently deleted after the retention period expires
- Your admin can configure whether the Recycle Bin retains items for 7, 14, 30, or 60 days

The Recycle Bin protects the workbook itself—if you delete it entirely, you can bring it back.

Think of this as your centralized command hub for managing all data assets across your Tableau environment. While this tool is mainly used by Admins, it will come in handy for Data Stewards as well.

The Data Orchestration Center brings together previously scattered governance tools into one unified interface, allowing you to:

- Centrally manage all data assets – from data sources and Prep flows to extracts and semantic models
- Centralize oversight of data lineage, quality, and usage patterns
- Strengthen data trust through streamlined data preparation monitoring
- Monitor data health across your entire Tableau ecosystem from a single dashboard

For data governance practitioners, this is a gamechanger. Instead of jumping between different pages to check data source status, extract refresh health, and lineage information, you now have a single pane of glass. This directly supports the GDPR principle of accountability by making it easier to demonstrate control over your data assets.

## Certifying data sources

Certifying a data source involves designating it as a trusted and reliable asset through a process that verifies its quality, accuracy, and compliance. This certification instills trust, ensures data quality, supports compliance, promotes consistency, strengthens data governance, enhances decision-making, facilitates data discovery, and is conducted by data governance teams or experts within an organization.

You can certify a data source in Tableau by following these steps:

- 1. Open Tableau Server or Tableau Cloud
- 2. Open a data source
- 3. Click on the three dots next to the data source name
- 4. Select **Edit Certification**...

![](img/66cf6b89349fda3581a4f196cb3c98a4_710_0.png)

Figure 16.3: Certification

- 5. You can now type a message visible to data source users stating the reason for the certification or similar. What you type depends on the policies agreed on in your firm. Click **Save**.

![](img/66cf6b89349fda3581a4f196cb3c98a4_711_0.png)

Figure 16.4: Certification message

Next to the data source owner and date, you will now see an indication that this data source is certified:

![](img/66cf6b89349fda3581a4f196cb3c98a4_711_1.png)

Figure 16.5: Certified

Certified data sources help decision-making, enabling analysts and business users to have heightened confidence in the data they utilize, resulting in more precise insights, well-informed decisions, and enhanced business outcomes.

## Data quality warnings

Data quality warnings are notifications that highlight potential issues or anomalies in dataset quality, triggered by incomplete or missing data, inconsistencies, outliers, data integrity problems, validation failures, or outdated information. These warnings signal the need for further investigation and data cleansing to ensure correct analysis and decision-making, emphasizing the importance of addressing data quality issues for reliable insights.

You can set up a data quality warning by following these steps:

- 1. Open Tableau Server or Tableau Cloud
- 2. Open a data source
- 3. Click on the three dots next to the data source name
- 4. Select **Quality Warning** and then choose between **Quality Warning...** or **Extract Refresh Monitoring...**

The Data Quality Warning pop-up will look like this:

![](img/66cf6b89349fda3581a4f196cb3c98a4_712_0.png)

Figure 16.6: Data Quality Warning

- 5. From here, select one of five labels from the dropdown **Select label**:

![](img/66cf6b89349fda3581a4f196cb3c98a4_713_0.png)

Figure 16.7: Select label

The **Extract Refresh Monitoring** pop-up will look very similar, without a dropdown, though:

![](img/66cf6b89349fda3581a4f196cb3c98a4_713_1.png)

Figure 16.8: Extract Refresh Monitoring

In both cases, a Warning indicator will appear on the data source overview page:

![](img/66cf6b89349fda3581a4f196cb3c98a4_714_0.png)

Figure 16.9: Warning

If a schedule extraction fails, you can also check the Schedules overview page:

![](img/66cf6b89349fda3581a4f196cb3c98a4_714_1.png)

Figure 16.10: Schedules

Data quality warnings serve as indicators that data may require further investigation or cleaning before it can be used for analysis, reporting, or decision-making. They help find potential data issues that could affect the accuracy, reliability, and validity of analytical results. Addressing data quality warnings often involves data cleansing, error correction, and ensuring the data aligns with the required quality standards.

It is important to note that data quality warnings should be acted upon, as they can affect the reliability and validity of insights derived from the data. Resolving data quality issues and keeping high-quality data is crucial to making informed decisions and drawing exact conclusions from data analysis.

## Tableau lineage

Data lineage is important as it establishes trust in data, aids in data quality management and issue resolution, facilitates impact analysis, supports compliance with regulations, strengthens data governance and management practices, assists in troubleshooting and debugging, and enables successful data and system integration. Data lineage provides transparency, understanding, and control over data flows, contributing to improved data governance and decision-making processes.

To see the lineage, you can open any type of overview page—data sources, prep flows, sheets, workbooks, and others—on Tableau Server or Tableau Cloud and check the Lineage tab:

![](img/66cf6b89349fda3581a4f196cb3c98a4_715_0.png)

Figure 16.11: Tableau lineage
