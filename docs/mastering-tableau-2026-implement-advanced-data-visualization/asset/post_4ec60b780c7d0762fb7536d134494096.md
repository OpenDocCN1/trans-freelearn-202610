## Extending Tableau mapping with another technology

Next, we will consider how to extend Tableau's mapping capabilities with other tools. Tableau's developers were careful to create a mapping interface that is readily extensible. Some areas of this extensibility, such as connecting to a Web Map Service (WMS) server, are available directly from the interface; we'll explore that next.

### Using custom maps with a WMS

The easiest way to bring a custom map into Tableau is directly from Desktop. We need a properly formatted URL that points to a WMS server. Tableau Desktop can connect to any WMS server that supports the WMS 1.0.0, 1.1.0, or 1.1.1 standard.

A good place to find a list of such URLs is online directories that provide information on hundreds of different mapping services from around the world. Many government agencies, space agencies, and environmental organizations offer free WMS services.

Why would you want to use a WMS map?

Imagine you own an online store. You could check if the sale of specific products is higher in certain areas—for example, umbrellas in rainy regions or sun protection in hotter areas. By adding a weather WMS layer, you can see if there's a correlation between local conditions and your sales. Or perhaps you need to monitor wildfire risks near your warehouses, or track sea ice for shipping routes. WMS maps bring this kind of contextual, real-world data directly into your Tableau dashboards.

### What to look for when choosing a WMS URL:

- **Reliability**: Is the service hosted by a stable organization (government, university, space agency)?
- **Update frequency**: Does the data update in real-time, daily, or is it a static historical layer?
- **Geographic coverage**: Does the service cover your area of interest (global, regional, local)?
- **Image format**: Does the server return standard formats like PNG or JPEG?
- **License**: Is the service free for commercial use, or only for educational/personal use?
