## Foreword

Generative AI has made many people nervous about tools like Tableau. "Won't AI just build dashboards for us?" I hear it constantly. And honestly, for simple charts, maybe it will. But data visualization was never really about building charts. It's about communication. It's about understanding what someone needs to see, why they need to see it, and what they'll do once they've seen it. AI can generate a bar chart. It can't sit in a stakeholder meeting and figure out which questions actually matter. That kind of judgment takes practice. It takes structured learning. And that's what this book is for.

Mastering Tableau covers a genuinely wide range. You start where you'd expect: worksheets, dashboards, data connections, the continuous/discrete distinction that trips up more people than it should. But it doesn't stay there. The chapters on data preparation and Tableau Prep Builder are a useful reminder that the work before the visualization often determines whether the analysis holds up when someone inevitably asks a question you didn't plan for. Joins, blends, relationships, scaffolding: these get proper treatment here, not just a passing mention.

The table calculations chapter is one I'd particularly point people toward. Partitioning and addressing logic is one of those topics that most Tableau users know exists, but few feel truly comfortable with. The approach here, building intuition with a playground across one, two, and three dimensions, is how I wish more people learned it. Level of Detail calculations get similar depth. FIXED, INCLUDE, and EXCLUDE, all grounded in Tableau's order of operations, which matters far more than most people realize.

Then there are the topics that many Tableau resources quietly skip. Data densification. Domain completion and padding. Custom polygons. Spatial joins. Integrating R and Python. Performance tuning. There's even a section on analyzing a chess game in Tableau, which is the kind of creative stretch that reminds you how flexible the tool actually is.

The design chapters pull it all together: formatting rules, color theory, layout patterns, and a walkthrough of every action type from filter actions through to set actions. And the closing sections on Tableau Server, Cloud, and data governance make sure you're thinking about how your work lives beyond your desktop.

I've made over 600 Tableau videos. A quick tutorial can get you started. But if you want to understand the tool, have the intuition to know how and when to use the product and when not to, you need something more thorough.

This is that book.

*Tim Ngwena*

Content Creator, Five-Time Tableau Visionary and Analytics Consultant
