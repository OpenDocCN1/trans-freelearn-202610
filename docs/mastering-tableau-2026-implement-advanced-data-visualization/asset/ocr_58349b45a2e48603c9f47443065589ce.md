# Utilizing OData, Data Densification, Big Data, and Google BigQuery

Many questions that Tableau newbies have are not related to data preparation, joins, unions, data blending, or data structures, but rather, how Tableau behaves in situations that often confuse beginners, such as unexpected nulls, unfamiliar table-calculation results, or performance concerns with large datasets. We'll cover such situations in this chapter, and you'll also gain a clearer understanding of concepts such as data densification, domain padding, and how Tableau interacts with modern data platforms.

The goal of this chapter is to build your confidence in interpreting Tableau's behavior by explaining *why* certain outcomes appear in your views and *how* Tableau manages data behind the scenes. By exploring connectors, densification techniques, and big data workflows, you'll be better equipped to troubleshoot issues and design more reliable visualizations.

New Tableau users often assume that confusing results are caused by data quality problems, when in reality they come from how Tableau structures and queries data. Understanding these mechanics will help you avoid common pitfalls, accurately diagnose unexpected results, and make informed decisions when working with both small and large datasets.

In this chapter, we will discuss the following topics:

+   - Using the OData connector
+   - Introducing data densification
+   - Tableau and big data
