# The paradigm shift

To master Tableau's new features, we must first understand the philosophical shift they represent. Traditional BI, even with beautiful dashboards, is fundamentally reactive. A user must log in, navigate to a dashboard, apply filters, and visually search for outliers or trends. It relies on the user knowing what to look for.

Agentic analytics inverts this model. It is proactive, personalized, and autonomous. Agentic analytics functions as a continuous monitoring system, detecting significant shifts and investigating root causes without manual intervention. They detect a significant shift, investigate the root cause by segmenting the data, and deliver a plain-language summary of the finding, complete with an explanation, directly to your inbox, Slack channel, or Salesforce console. This is at least the promise of Tableau's AI evolution.

This shift is built on three core pillars, which we will explore in detail:

+   1. A unified, trusted data layer (Tableau Semantics)
+   2. Proactive, personalized insights delivered at scale (Tableau Pulse)
+   3. Autonomous agents that can analyze, reason, and trigger actions (Tableau Next)

# Understanding the Tableau and Salesforce Ecosystem

As we dive into Tableau's AI-powered features, you may encounter some confusion about which products belong where. Is Tableau Pulse part of Tableau or Salesforce? What about Tableau Next? And why do some features require Salesforce licenses? Let me explain.

To understand the current product landscape, we need to go back to June 2019, when Salesforce acquired Tableau for $15.7 billion. At the time, this was one of the largest acquisitions in Salesforce's history, and it signaled a clear strategic direction: Tableau would become the analytics brand for the entire Salesforce ecosystem.

The vision behind this integration was ambitious: combine Tableau's best-in-class visualization with Salesforce's AI capabilities (Einstein) and customer data. This vision has gradually materialized, and today Tableau products exist across two interconnected ecosystems. Understanding this distinction is crucial for knowing which features you can access and what licenses you need.

On the one hand we have the original Tableau Desktop, Tableau Prep and Tableau Server (also Tableau Cloud and Tableau Public) products in the Tableau ecosystem and Tableau Next, Tableau Semantics and Agentforce in the Salesforce ecosystem. Tableau Next represents a significant evolution. Unlike Tableau Core, which often requires switching between Desktop, Prep, and Server depending on your task, Tableau Next is designed as a unified, browser-based experience where you can perform all analytics tasks in one place.

It is built entirely on the Salesforce platform and includes Agentforce Tableau with AI agents like Concierge and Inspector, Tableau Semantics and Data 360, Salesforce's unified data platform (formerly Data Cloud)

It is widely expected that Tableau Next will eventually replace Tableau Core (Desktop, Prep, and Server), though Tableau has stated that classic products remain core investments for the foreseeable future.
