+   - **One-to-many cardinality**: This is the most common type of relationship, where one entity on one side participates with many entities on the other side.

An example is the relation **Company** produces **Car**: a company produces many cars, and a car is produced by exactly one company. The **Person** owns **Car** relationship shown later might be a good example of one-to-many, depending on the semantics of **Person** and *owns*.

![](bbox=[0.295, 0.315, 0.705, 0.375])

Figure 3.6: E-R sample model for one-to-many cardinality

+   - **Many-to-many cardinality**: This is a relatively uncommon case where many entities on one side participate in the relationship with many entities on the other side.

An example is the **Student** follows **Course** relationship: a student follows one or more courses, otherwise loses their student status, and a course is followed by zero or more students.
