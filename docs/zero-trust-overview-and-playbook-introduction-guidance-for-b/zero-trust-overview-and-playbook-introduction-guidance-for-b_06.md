这与当今云责任的管理方式非常相似（许多 AI 提供商也是云提供商）。这也类似于一家企业将其制造、物流、销售（例如渠道销售）或其他业务职能的部分或全部外包。

## AI 将如何影响零信任？

AI 将加速零信任的许多方面，因为它极大地改善了安全工具以及人们使用它们的能力。AI 承诺减少对重要但繁琐的安全任务的负担和工作量，例如以下内容：

- 帮助安全分析师快速查询多个数据源（无需成为查询语言或工具界面的专家）
- 帮助撰写事件响应报告
- 识别常见的后续行动以防止重复事件

简化人员与他们为安全所需使用的复杂系统之间的界面，将使技能各异的人员更具生产力。技能高超的人员能够在没有重复且分散注意力的任务的情况下，专注于他们最擅长的工作。职业生涯早期的人能够迅速在角色中变得更具生产力，更快地以专家水平执行任务，并通过回答问题和提供解释来帮助他们学习。

*AI 不会取代对安全专家的需求，也不会取代对安全现代化的需求。* AI 将简化许多安全流程，并允许更少的安全人员做更多工作，但它不会取代安全思维或安全专业知识的需求。

即使有了 AI 技术，对于以下方面仍然需要人员和流程：

- 向 AI 系统提出正确的安全问题
- 解释结果并评估其准确性
- 对 AI 结果采取行动并在团队间协调
- 执行 AI 系统目前无法覆盖的分析和任务：
  - 识别、管理和衡量组织的安全风险
  - 制定、执行和监控策略和政策
  - 建立并监控团队之间的关系和流程
  - 整合业务、技术和安全能力
  - 评估合规要求并确保组织在善意中满足这些要求
  - 评估业务和技术流程的安全性
  - 评估安全态势并优先考虑缓解投资
  - 评估安全流程、工具和系统的有效性
  - 为技术系统规划和实施安全
  - 为应用程序和产品规划和实施安全
  - 响应攻击并从攻击中恢复

总之，AI 将迅速改变您面临的攻击以及组织有效管理安全风险的能力。AI 需要零信任方法，它也将帮助您的团队更快、更高效地完成工作。

《零信任手册系列》中的指导将通过指导每个人完成各自的部分，加速您管理 AI 风险的能力。它将帮助您迅速将安全与业务风险和优先事项保持一致，并提供您有效管理 AI 变化所需的 安全敏捷性。

## AI 将如何影响零信任？

AI 将加速零信任的许多方面，因为它极大地改善了安全工具以及人们使用它们的能力。AI 承诺减少对重要但繁琐的安全任务的负担和工作量，例如以下内容：

- 帮助安全分析师快速查询多个数据源（无需成为查询语言或工具界面的专家）
- 帮助撰写事件响应报告
- 识别常见的后续行动以防止重复事件

简化人员与他们为安全所需使用的复杂系统之间的界面，将使技能各异的人员更具生产力。技能高超的人员能够在没有重复且分散注意力的任务的情况下，专注于他们最擅长的工作。职业生涯早期的人能够迅速在角色中变得更具生产力，更快地以专家水平执行任务，并通过回答问题和提供解释来帮助他们学习。

*AI 不会取代对安全专家的需求，也不会取代对安全现代化的需求。* AI 将简化许多安全流程，并允许更少的安全人员做更多工作，但它不会取代安全思维或安全专业知识的需求。

即使有了 AI 技术，对于以下方面仍然需要人员和流程：

- 向 AI 系统提出正确的安全问题
- 解释结果并评估其准确性
- 对 AI 结果采取行动并在团队间协调
- 执行 AI 系统目前无法覆盖的分析和任务：
  - 识别、管理和衡量组织的安全风险
  - 制定、执行和监控策略和政策
  - 建立并监控团队之间的关系和流程
  - 整合业务、技术和安全能力
  - 评估合规要求并确保组织在善意中满足这些要求
  - 评估业务和技术流程的安全性
  - 评估安全态势并优先考虑缓解投资
  - 评估安全流程、工具和系统的有效性
  - 为技术系统规划和实施安全
  - 为应用程序和产品规划和实施安全
  - 响应攻击并从攻击中恢复

总之，AI 将迅速改变您面临的攻击以及组织有效管理安全风险的能力。AI 需要零信任方法，它也将帮助您的团队更快、更高效地完成工作。

## 追求完美解决方案是完美的浪费
在安全领域不存在单一的“银弹”解决方案可以解决所有问题（尽管任何安全厂商可能声称 ☺）。
经典安全方法通常以合规的完美终态、完美的网络配置或“完美的新工具”修复一切作为理想终态。监管标准无法跟上攻击者，网络安全边界不足，单一工具或技术无法阻止决心的网络对手。
*构建安全韧性是一段多步骤、多学习的旅程，而不是飞往预定目的地的单程航班。* 虽然我们都希望安全有简单的捷径，但我们所防御的业务和技术环境是复杂的。没有任何单一解决方案能让业务资产免受每个创造性攻击者及其学习/演进的侵害。



我们需要提出那些真正重要且困难的问题（这些问题往往没有清晰、简单的答案），并专注于回答它们所需要的艰苦工作（这通常涉及创造力）。试图把问题重新塑造成与我们已知的解决方案相匹配，往往只会产生浪费的努力。

安全敏捷有助于建立韧性，并使你通过学习和适应每天变得更强，在每次失败后也更强（有时被称为反脆弱性）。

## 是大刀阔斧更好还是规划更小的项目？

人们经常询问是否需要大型项目才能从零信任中获得价值，还是较小的项目（们）有效。

从赞助和战略规划的角度来看，*你应该尽可能做大*。这是什么意思？使其具有战略性，将其与公司的技术、业务和运营模式以及路线图保持一致，并理想情况下获得高层赞助。由组织领导赞助并与业务目标和风险保持一致的战略性零信任举措，比小型、分散的技术项目更能有效交付价值。

较小的零信任举措可以快速交付有意义的价值，但与跨团队的全面转型相比，较小的项目在能够交付的价值方面有限。要获得零信任转型的全部价值和收益，你需要一个由组织领导提供高层赞助的全面转型计划。

需要记住的是，*大型转型是一系列协调且有意的小型变更*（通常由路线图指导，如零信任的情况）。与每个大型转型一样，零信任最终是一系列与更大战略目标保持一致的小型决策和行动。操作手册将此转型分解为可操作的要素，可由每个角色执行，通常彼此独立。

无论零信任项目或举措的规模如何，你都应 *专注于以小步增量进行规划和执行，并在过程中进行调整*，以便保持对不断变化需求的敏捷性。

规划零信任举措（们）的规模和范围时，关键考虑因素如下：

-   **大型零信任转型是最有效的**，并且需要高层赞助。
-   **良好的沟通可以催化高层赞助**，这对大型转型是必需的。
-   **有时需要从小处着手**，但速度更慢且交付的价值更小。

让我们更深入地看看每个方面。

## 大型零信任转型是最有效的

零信任最有效的方法是对由组织首席执行官（或同等职位）赞助并由像零信任操作手册系列中嵌入的那样的敏捷战略路线图指导的大型转型的承诺。这种具有高层赞助和业务集成的全面转型使零信任能够充分实现所有承诺的收益。

完整的零信任转型需要在整个组织中进行协调、制定有意的计划并提供支持性的文化要素。与全面转型相比，在技术和安全团队内运营的小型举措影响有限。操作手册系列旨在带你完成整个流程。

## 良好的沟通可以催化高层赞助

对零信任（或总体安全）缺乏高层赞助通常是由认知、理解和沟通方面的差距驱动的，而非组织领导者缺乏兴趣。

沟通差距通常由缺乏认知和理解驱动：

-   **业务领导者**：大多数组织领导者关心业务运营和知识产权的风险，但不明确如何评估、判断和管理安全风险。有些人甚至错误地认为安全只是一个“技术问题”，技术团队应该能够在没有业务领导参与的情况下处理（与由智能人类罪犯和其他行为者驱动的动态风险相对）。
  这些理解差距仅仅是因为大多数业务领导者没有直接的安全经验，也没有接受过如何在其角色中有效管理安全风险的正式培训。
-   **安全领导者**：许多安全领导者可能不熟悉业务术语和流程，导致他们使用自己熟悉的技术术语和框架来传达安全风险。这种方法对业务领导者来说不熟悉且令人困惑，往往强化了安全只是一个技术问题的错误信念。

这种动态使得业务领导者难以准确判断安全风险并与安全领导者进行富有成效的安全风险讨论，从而导致对零信任或整体安全的赞助不足。

操作手册通过为每个利益相关者的角色和视角提供明确指导来帮助克服这些差距。这建立了基础理解，有助于催化

富有成效的对话和协作关于安全风险。在当今不断变化的世界中总有更多需要学习的内容，但这些手册将有助于启动积极的循环。

## 有时需要从小处着手

规模较小的零信任计划在价值方面存在局限，但有时你别无选择，只能从小处着手来证明零信任，以便获得高层赞助。在这种情况下，你必须通过在理解零信任价值的团队内部启动规模更小、目标更明确的项目，来为零信任建立信誉和势头。这种有机“自下而上”的方法进展较慢，但仍比完全不采用零信任更有价值。这种方法要求在执行过程中更加注重传达零信任的价值。推动零信任项目（们）的团队必须定期向组织领导沟通零信任如何实现业务目标并创造价值。这有助于为额外的投资、业务整合以及完整零信任计划的赞助建立案例。

在这些情况下，主张零信任的团队必须专注于推动快速成果，这些成果能够立即交付并与传统安全方法相比显示出切实价值（例如使用便捷性提高、组织风险降低等）。团队通常必须通过在这些快速成果基础上持续取得增量进展来建立势头感。

你需要建立足够的零信任愿景，以指导团队在最初几个敏捷项目中开展工作。该愿景应尽可能基于组织的战略和方向来制定。

## 治理与敏捷之间的自然张力是健康的

安全是一个复杂的领域，因此某些决策需要平衡各种冲突的要求并做出判断。

*威胁环境的变化速度往往超过标准跟上的能力。*

合规导向的治理职能与实时攻击者演变之间始终存在且将一直存在自然张力，后者改变了哪些风险最重要（有时还会引入新风险）。

在保持一致以满足（大多静态的）监管标准的同时保持敏捷以跟上攻击者演变之间的这种张力必须不断管理。这些情况要求你考虑多个视角并做出最好的优先级决策（并预期你可能以后也需要改变它）。

**注意**

在许多方面，这种张力反映了许多电视节目和电影中“按部就班”角色与“凭直觉行事”角色之间的经典张力。

*合规和安全都是必要的，它们之间的张力对于推动知情和明智的决策是有价值的。*

合规和实时攻击者风险这两种视角都是有效且有价值的，但两者都不是真理的垄断者。如果不由另一方平衡，任何一方走极端都可能增加组织的风险。



Here are some examples to illustrate why both priorities have to be addressed in your roadmap:

- **What's old is new again:** Sometimes attackers find ways to exploit older techniques to get access to an organization's resources. Mitigating these requires applying well-known recommendations covered by compliance regulations such as installing security updates (sometimes called security patches) or applying well-known security configuration recommendations.

One example of this is the highly damaging WannaCry/WannaCrypt worm in 2017, which exploited organizations that didn't apply an update to fix a software vulnerability released by the vendor two months prior (and was advertised as urgent by many organizations and the press). Organizations that invested in this basic hygiene maintenance task would have mitigated a significant amount of this damage.

Zero Trust's asset-centric approach addresses this naturally by focusing on assets throughout their life cycle. These basic hygiene maintenance tasks are addressed as part of the **Asset Centricity** capability described in *Chapter 4, Standard Zero Trust Capabilities*.

- **Compliance can't keep up:** On the other side of the equation, we find that compliance standards often can't keep up with today's threats. Additionally, obsolete compliance standards can be harmful for a long period before they are updated.

For example, *password complexity* requirements were once thought to be the strongest account protection, and this requirement was included in many compliance standards.

Unfortunately, we have learned in today's world that *password complexity requirements actually weaken security* because they cause people to create repetitive, guessable passwords and do very little to protect against the most prevalent password attacks. Because of this, the industry has moved on to focus on other newer solutions such as **Multifactor Authentication (MFA)**, behavior analysis, and passwordless (which is actually easier to use as well). *Many regulations today still have explicit requirements for password complexity that waste resources, create user frustration, and increase risk.*

While compliance and security will be aligned most of the time, it's critical to recognize there are times when they aren't. You should expect these situations and make the best judgment call you can with the best information you have (including *security intelligence* that fuses business intelligence and threat intelligence insights).

## What is the best place to start Zero Trust?

Organizations may not be able to fully commit to all Zero Trust initiatives at once, so it’s important to ask what to prioritize first.

Organizations should always focus on quick wins that can show progress in a short amount of time and prioritize areas with the highest impact on the organization’s business initiatives and security risk.

What is most important will vary for each organization, but many organizations often start by prioritizing these Zero Trust capabilities:

- **Adaptive access**: Establishing strong modern access control that enforces MFA and Conditional Access to resources is often a top priority. This is critical because traditional network security perimeters can no longer provide this basic protection against current threats and for current assets.
- **Asset-centric security operations**: Establishing strong detection, response, and recovery capabilities for common modern attacks is often another top priority. This is critical because traditional approaches based on static query **Intrusion Detection Systems/ Intrusion Protection Systems (IDSs/IPSs)** and firewall logs are not effective against modern attack techniques on identity, endpoint, cloud, and other assets.
- **Asset-centric protection for cloud assets**: Organizations that have migrated or established new business-critical assets in the cloud must adopt modern data-centric and system asset-centric approaches to protect business-critical data and systems. Organizations often find that legacy network security perimeter approaches block core cloud functionality, which restricts business agility. Zero Trust enables organizations to protect cloud data and systems such as business websites and **Application Programming Interfaces (APIs)** without losing agility or security assurances.

Now let’s take a moment to provide clarity on some words that often cause confusion because they mean different things to different people.

## Key terminology changes and clarification

One of the side effects of connecting business, technology, and security teams together is terminology confusion. As these teams start working more closely together, they often face points of confusion around language, terminology, and its meaning.

Some terminology and concepts are new to teams that haven't worked together before, some of the same (or similar) terminology has different meanings to different teams, some terminology is new to everyone, and some common terminology is just outdated and misleading.

The most common points of confusion typically center around the use of the words *assets* (and related words), *operations* (and related words), and *the network* (or *the enterprise*).

## Newer terminology – technical estate

The series uses “technical estate” or “technology estate” to refer to all technology used by an organization to get work done, including all data and all IT, Operational Technology (OT), and Internet of Things (IoT) systems.

This newer term replaces the use of outdated and misleading terms including *the network* or *the enterprise* to refer to all organizational technical resources.

The books avoid using *the network* because this term is both of the following:

- **Wrong**: The organization's assets are no longer hosted exclusively on their private network(s).
- **Distracting**: Protecting a network takes focus away from the assets of different values that may (or may not) be on it.

The book series also avoids using *the enterprise* because it implies that Zero Trust only applies to larger “enterprise” organizations when Zero Trust is equally important to smaller organizations.

These older terms also imply that all work is done on the organization’s own equipment and networks, which is no longer true. The technology estate that can impact business risk now includes data and systems owned and operated by the organization, partners, cloud providers, software suppliers, government regulators, employees and contractors (personal devices), and others.

## Disambiguation – operations, operational, operating model, and so on

This model and the playbook series contain multiple variations of the word operations. These words are used extensively throughout business, financial, technology, and security teams. These variations all share a common theme to reference something about the operational phase of business, technology, or security, but each has evolved to a specific meaning or aspect of operations.

This is a quick summary of the main variations seen in practice and in the playbooks:

- **Operational**: This business term refers to the daily business operations and activity of the entire organization producing products and services. This is also the name of one pillar in the three-pillar model used in the playbooks, which is described more in *Chapter 8, Adoption with the Three-Pillar Model*.
- **Operating model**: This business term refers to *the way that the organization operates*, the abstract model under which all operations and processes are organized. This big-picture view enables you to see the organization as a whole and provide good oversight and governance over all of it. This is also the name of a pillar in the three-pillar model, which is described more in *Chapter 8, Adoption with the Three-Pillar Model*.
- **Operating Expenses (OpEx)** (and **Capital Expenditures (CapEx)**): OpEx is a financial term referring to day-to-day expenses that occur as the business operates. This contrasts with CapEx, which refers to major purchases that often involve extensive planning ahead of time. Many IT and security costs are shifting to an OpEx mode from a CapEx mode because cloud services have a "pay for what you use" dynamic instead of larger one-time purchases of hardware and software licenses for on-premises technology. Additionally, this fundamentally changes the nature of how technology is financed and reported because OpEx is accounted for as a short-term budget expense while CapEx results in financial "assets" that must be amortized or depreciated over time. Because of how fast things change in the digital era, an OpEx model is often preferable because it is more flexible and because it avoids financial reporting issues where large capital investments (CapEx) become obsolete before their planned amortization and depreciation dates.
- **IT operations**: This primarily refers to the organizational function (and team) that implements, operates, and maintains IT. Sometimes this may refer to all operational functions of IT, and sometimes to a specific part such as service desk and user support functions (separate from an infrastructure operations team) or traditional on-premises teams (separate from the cloud service operations team).
- **OT**: This is a category of computing technology at the level of **IT** and **IoT** classifications. OT refers to computer systems that control or monitor physical processes, such as computer control of manufacturing, windmills, fluid valves, power generation and distribution, and others. This is sometimes also referred to as **Industrial Control Systems (ICSs)** or **Supervisory Control and Data Acquisition (SCADA)**.
- **Security Operations (SecOps)**: This is sometimes referred to as a SOC (short for security operations center). This security term refers to how your organization manages attacks by detecting, responding to, and recovering from incidents (caused by attackers). This refers to both the name of the security discipline (and associated processes) and is often also the name of the team performing that function.




> **注意**
> SecOps 并不是唯一的运营安全职能；安全态势管理是一种互补职能，专注于防止攻击。这两个职能将在 *第 8 章，采用三支柱模型* 中得到更详细的描述。
