Now, let's see what Fermat's Last Theorem is, where it is applied, and why it is useful for us. Fermat's Last Theorem is one of the best and most beautiful theorems of classical mathematics strictly related to prime numbers. According to Wikipedia, "In number theory, Fermat's Last Theorem (sometimes called Fermat's conjecture, especially in older texts) states that no three positive integers a, b, and c satisfy the equation a^n + b^n = c^n for any integer value of n greater than 2. The cases n = 1 and n = 2 have been known since antiquity to have infinitely many solutions." In other words, it tells us that given the following equation, for any exponent, **n>3**, ≥ 3 there is no integer, **a**, **b**, or **c**, that verifies the sum: a^n+b^n = c^n Why is this theorem so important for us? Firstly, it's because Fermat's Last Theorem is strictly related to prime numbers. In fact, given the properties of primes, in order to demonstrate Fermat's Last Theorem, it's sufficient to demonstrate the following: a^p+b^p ≠ c^p Here, **p** is any prime number greater than 2.
