To perform the bit encryption, **E**, we use the **XOR** function between **R<sub>i-1</sub> = (100110)<sub>2</sub>** expanded and **K<sub>i</sub> = (01100101)<sub>2</sub>**. I call this output **E(K<sub>i</sub>)**: Exp(R<sub>i−1</sub>) K<sub>i</sub> = 10101010 01100101 = E(K<sub>i</sub>) (11001111). At this point, we split **E(K<sub>i</sub>)**, consisting of 8 bits, into two parts: a 4-bit half for the left and a 4-bit half for the right: L(EK<sub>i</sub>) = (1100)<sub>2</sub>, R(EK<sub>i</sub>) = (1111)<sub>2</sub>. Now, we process the 4 bits to the left and the 4 bits to the right with two S-Box 2×8 matrices consisting of 3 bits for each element. The input, as I mentioned earlier, is 4 bits: the first one is the row and the last three represent a binary number to indicate the column (the same as previously, just with fewer bits). So, 0 stands for first and 1 stands for second. Similarly, 000 stands for the first column, 001 stands for the second column, and so on until 111. We call the two S-Boxes S1 and S2. **L(E(K<sub>i</sub>)) = (1100)<sub>2</sub>** is processed by **S1**; so, the element of the second row, **(1)<sub>2</sub>**, and the fourth column, **(100)<sub>2</sub>**, is the output, here represented by the number **(000)<sub>2</sub>**. **R(E(K<sub>i</sub>)) = (1111)<sub>2</sub>** is processed by **S2**; so, the element of the second row, **(1)<sub>2</sub>**, and the seventh column, **(111)<sub>2</sub>**, is the output, here represented by the number **(100)<sub>2</sub>**. Now, the last step is the concatenation of the two outputs obtained, here expressed by the notation ∥, which will perform the ciphertext: S1(L(E(K<sub>i</sub>))) = (000)<sub>2</sub> ∥ S2(R(E(K<sub>i</sub>))) = (100)<sub>2</sub>.
