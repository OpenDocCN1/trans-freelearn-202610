# Figure 2.19 – The Enable Content prompt

If you poke around in the **Trust Center** settings in Excel, you can disable all of these prompts across
the board. However, doing so presents a higher level of risk than simply disabling **Protected View**.
When you disable **Protected View**, the **Enable Content** prompts still appear, creating a gauntlet of
prompts. Additionally, the **Enable Content** prompt can be triggered by workbook links and workbooks
that have macros. If you’ve created the external connections, workbook links, or macros, then there’s
no risk, and you can safely turn these prompts off by creating a trusted document.

> > **Nuance**
> > If you click on the **Enable Content** button in Excel’s **Message Bar**, you will relegate yourself to
> having to click on that button each time you open your file. Do not click on **Enable Content**
> if you wish to suppress the prompt; instead, use that prompt as a tickler to carry out the steps
> in the next section.

If you wish to suppress the **Enable Content** prompt for a specific workbook, carry out these steps
when that button is present on screen:

+   1. Choose **File | Info**.
+   2. Click on **Enable Content | Enable All Content**.
