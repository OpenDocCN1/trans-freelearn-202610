![](bbox=[0.1484, 0.1703, 0.8711, 0.4531])

**Figure 7.30 – The AutoFill Options command**

> > **Nuance**
> > If you prefer to paste the data as a normal range of cells, choose **Paste Options | Values and Number Formatting** (the second command in the **Paste Values** section). However, you then cannot apply the column widths through the **Paste Options** button—doing so will convert the data back into a **Table**. Instead, copy the original **Table** again, select the first cell in the data you pasted, choose **Home | Paste | Paste Special | Column Widths**, and then click on **OK**.

# ### Selecting a portion of a Table

If you copy a portion of a **Table**, you will paste a normal range of cells that masquerades as a **Table**:

+   1. Copy cells B4:F14 in the **Feature Comparison** worksheet, such that you’re copying everything except row 15.

+   2. Create a new workbook, and after you paste the data, notice that the cells look like a **Table** but are a normal range of cells masquerading as a **Table**.

> > **Tip**
> > The presence or lack of the **Table Design** tab is categorical evidence as to whether a range of cells is an actual **Table** or imposter cells.
